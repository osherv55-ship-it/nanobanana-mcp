# Labs: Bachmeyer's "optimal" targets vs standard ranges

**Scope.** This covers every lab marker Dr. Trevor Bachmeyer uses in the research corpus. For each one it gives his stated target, the standard reference range or guideline cutoff, a judgment of where his target holds up and where it is stricter than the evidence, the problems he uses it for, and whether it is available in Israel.

**Provenance tags.**
- **[his framing]**: what he says. A number or phrase shown in quotation marks was found word for word in the normalized raw transcript corpus ("[his framing, verified]" marks the ones re-checked for this version). Unverified wording has no quotation marks and is marked [paraphrase].
- **[literature]**: guideline or study consensus.
- **[inferred]**: the analyst's reasoning, or something not checked against a source.
- **[verified]** on an Israeli kupah item: seen on that kupah's own web page during this session. The URL is listed in §5 Sources.
- **[critic]**: taken from the critics' audit in the corpus.

**Hard boundary.** This document has no doses, routes, frequencies or titration rules for any peptide, research compound, prescription drug or hormone. Several of his lab thresholds are tied in his videos to drug changes: hCG, enclomiphene, aromatase inhibitors, kisspeptin, retatrutide, and glucose-lowering medication. Those actions, and the trigger values he attaches to them, are **deliberately withheld here**. He links certain glucose values to medication changes; that content is withheld. Any glucose result while on glucose-lowering drugs goes to the prescriber (see rule #4). Every rule in §3 ends in a safe action: retest, change foundations, stop a non-prescribed product, see the physician, or get emergency care.

## 0. His lab philosophy in five lines, and the reality check

| His position | Verified wording | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Reference ranges are population averages, not health | "your lab reference ranges are a lie", "that's not optimal that's a reference range", "reference ranges are disgusting" [his framing] | **Partly true.** Some ranges are built from unscreened populations, so they include sick people. ALT is the best-documented case: ACG 2017 lowered the "true healthy" upper limit to about 33 U/L for men and 25 U/L for women. But most guideline cutoffs (glucose, HbA1c, LDL, UACR, eGFR) are **outcome-based, not population averages**. Going below them has no trial evidence of benefit [literature]. |
| Order a wide panel before doing anything | "without knowing your fasting insulin or ApoB" you're "blind firing" [his framing, verified] | Measuring before acting is sound. Wide panels with no pre-test question produce false positives and cascades of further testing (Choosing Wisely) [literature]. |
| Watch trends, not one snapshot | "time lapse labs": repeat over a week or two and watch the trend [his framing] | **Defensible.** Biological variation is real. Guidelines require repeats before diagnosis: two morning testosterone samples, two hs-CRP samples about 2 weeks apart, UACR positive in 2 of 3 samples over 3–6 months [literature]. |
| HbA1c and TSH are "downstream" and misleading | "it's three months looking behind you", "you can't use TSH", "it's a bunky worthless number" [his framing] | HbA1c is the outcome-validated diabetes marker. TSH is the guideline first-line thyroid screen (ATA/ETA). Both have known blind spots, listed below, but neither is "worthless" [literature]. |
| He gives conflicting numbers across videos | See the flags in each row | Treat his numbers as **personal targets, not consensus**. The critics' audit in the corpus says the same: "his personal thresholds, framed as absolutes, not guideline values" [literature/critic]. |

**Units trap: read this before comparing any number.** He speaks in US units. Israeli printouts vary by kupah and lab. Only Clalit's CRP unit was checked on a kupah page [verified]; the other Israeli units below are [inferred]. Always read the unit printed next to the value.

| Marker | US unit he uses | Possible Israeli unit | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRP / hs-CRP | mg/L (assumed) | **mg/dL on Clalit's CRP page, normal 0–0.50 mg/dL** [verified: Clalit CRP page] | 1 mg/dL = 10 mg/L. A printout showing "0.4 mg/dL" equals 4 mg/L, which is **high** cardiovascular risk, not "under his 0.5". Clalit's printed upper limit "0.50" looks identical to his "0.5" but is 10 times larger (5 mg/L). |
| 25(OH) vitamin D | ng/mL | ng/mL or nmol/L | nmol/L ÷ 2.5 = ng/mL |
| Insulin | µIU/mL (= mU/L) | µIU/mL or pmol/L | pmol/L ÷ 6 = µIU/mL |
| Testosterone | ng/dL | ng/dL or nmol/L | nmol/L × 28.8 = ng/dL |
| Cortisol (serum) | µg/dL | µg/dL or nmol/L | nmol/L ÷ 27.6 = µg/dL |
| Free T4 / Free T3 | ng/dL / pg/mL | pmol/L | FT4 pmol/L ÷ 12.87; FT3 pmol/L ÷ 1.536 |

## 2. Standard panels per problem, with recheck cadence

All panels below are **[literature]-based** unless tagged. Bachmeyer extras worth discussing with the physician are marked ➕. Extras with weak value are marked ➖.

### 2.1 Insulin resistance / prediabetes / T2D
- **Baseline:** fasting glucose, fasting insulin → HOMA-IR, HbA1c, lipid panel (TG, HDL, LDL) → TG/HDL, ALT/AST/GGT, creatinine/eGFR, UACR (if diabetes or hypertension), CBC, ferritin, B12 (if on metformin).
 - ➕ ApoB. ➕ Uric acid. ➖ IL-6/TNF-α. ➖ RBC magnesium.
- **Recheck:**
 - After a lifestyle change: fasting glucose, insulin and HOMA-IR at **12 weeks**. This is when his "90-day" window and the physiology agree [inferred].
 - Diabetes: HbA1c every **3 months** until at goal, then every **6 months** (ADA).
 - Prediabetes: **yearly**.
 - Use the same lab for insulin each time [inferred].

### 2.2 Liver (MASLD / "fatty liver")
- **Baseline:** ALT, AST, GGT, ALP, total and direct bilirubin, albumin, CBC (platelets) → **FIB-4**, fasting glucose/insulin, HbA1c, lipids, ferritin plus **transferrin saturation**, hepatitis B/C serology, alcohol history.
 - ➕ Ultrasound, and elastography if FIB-4 ≥1.3 (AASLD 2023 / EASL 2024).
 - INR and albumin matter only if there is advanced disease.
- **Recheck:**
 - Liver enzymes and the metabolic panel **3–6 months** after a lifestyle change.
 - FIB-4 every **1–3 years** depending on risk (AASLD 2023).
 - Imaging per hepatologist.

### 2.3 Cardiovascular risk
- **Baseline:** lipid panel, **ApoB**, **Lp(a) once in a lifetime**, hs-CRP (two samples about 2 weeks apart, never during illness), HbA1c/fasting glucose, creatinine/eGFR, UACR, blood pressure, smoking and family history.
 - ➕ hs-CRP, as an addition to LDL/ApoB and not a replacement. ➖ LDL particle size.
- **Recheck:**
 - Lipids **4–12 weeks** after any lipid therapy is started or changed, then every **3–12 months** (ACC/AHA 2018).
 - Lifestyle-only: **3–6 months**, then yearly.

### 2.4 Thyroid
- **Baseline:** **TSH first.** FT4 if TSH is abnormal, symptoms are strong, or pituitary disease is suspected. TPO antibodies if TSH is high. Thyroglobulin antibodies if TPO is negative and suspicion is high.
 - ➖ FT3 (except hyperthyroidism). ➖ Reverse T3.
- **Recheck:**
 - Abnormal TSH: repeat in **6–12 weeks** before labelling.
 - On thyroid medication: TSH **6–8 weeks** after any physician-made change, then every **6–12 months**.
 - During active dieting or GLP-1 weight loss, a low-normal FT3 is expected. Interpret only with the physician [literature/inferred].

### 2.5 Male hormones (suspected low T, or on TRT)
- **Diagnosis:** total testosterone on **two separate mornings (7–10 AM, fasting)**, then LH, FSH, prolactin, SHBG → calculated free T, CBC/hematocrit, ferritin plus transferrin saturation. PSA by age and shared decision. Fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids.
 - ➕ Sensitive estradiol (LC-MS/MS) when relevant.
- **On TRT** (Endocrine Society 2018): testosterone and hematocrit at **3–6 months**, then **yearly**. PSA per guideline. Blood pressure.
- **Stopping TRT** is a physician-planned process. Labs alone do not license a self-run "restart".

### 2.6 Menopause / perimenopause
- **Diagnosis:** clinical if age ≥45 with typical symptoms; FSH is **not required** (NAMS/NICE). FSH/estradiol only if under 45 or the picture is unclear.
- **Baseline:** lipids, ApoB (➕), fasting glucose/HbA1c, TSH if symptomatic, CBC/ferritin if bleeding is heavy, blood pressure, mammography per screening, bone density by age and risk.
- **On HRT:** **no routine estradiol level monitoring** for standard transdermal regimens. Review yearly, and evaluate any unscheduled bleeding.
 - Oral estrogen raises CRP, SHBG and liver-made clotting factors such as fibrinogen; transdermal estrogen largely does not, and observational data link it to lower venous-clot risk. His point here is true. Which route fits a given woman is **a decision for a physician** (§1L) [literature].
 - His panel here is empty ("get labs" with nothing specified, per the critic audit) [critic].

### 2.7 Kidney (CKD risk: diabetes, hypertension, family history)
- **Baseline:** creatinine/eGFR, **UACR**, potassium, bicarbonate, CBC, urinalysis.
 - ➕ Cystatin C if creatinine is unreliable (high muscle mass, creatine supplements, amputation, very low weight) (KDIGO 2024).
 - G3 or worse: calcium, phosphate, PTH, 25(OH)D.
- **Recheck:**
 - At-risk: **yearly** eGFR and UACR.
 - Established CKD: **1–4× per year** depending on the KDIGO GFR × albuminuria grid.
 - Recheck creatinine sooner after vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration, or after starting kidney-active medicines.
- **⚠ Protein:** he claims KDIGO recommends 1 g protein per pound and advises raising protein in advanced CKD. This is false. KDOQI 2020: 0.55–0.6 g/kg (non-diabetic, CKD 3–5) or 0.6–0.8 g/kg (diabetic); KDIGO 2024: 0.8 g/kg, and avoid >1.3 g/kg in CKD G3–G5. **Never raise protein with eGFR <60 without the nephrologist** (see §1I) [literature].

### 2.8 GLP-1 / GIP medicine monitoring (approved agents only)
- **Baseline:** HbA1c, fasting glucose, lipids, ALT/AST/GGT, creatinine/eGFR, UACR (diabetes), CBC, ferritin, B12, pregnancy plans. Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer, MEN2 or pancreatitis is a screening question for the prescriber. Eye exam if diabetic retinopathy is present (risk with rapid HbA1c drops).
 - ➕ Body composition: DEXA or waist measurement instead of the scale alone. His point here is defensible.
- **Recheck:**
 - **3 months**, then every **6–12 months**.
 - eGFR/electrolytes promptly if there is vomiting, diarrhea or poor intake.
 - **Lipase only if symptoms appear.** Severe persistent abdominal pain: go to the emergency department now (rule #31a).
 - Glucose logs if also on insulin or sulfonylureas (hypoglycemia risk). Any adjustment is a physician decision.
- **Unapproved or "research" products:** retatrutide is investigational and is not approved by the FDA, the EMA or the Israel MoH as of October 2026 (Lilly plans to file in Q1 2027). Products sold outside trials are unregulated research chemicals. No lab panel makes an unverified product safe. That is a decision for a physician.

## 3. If/then interpretation rules (each ends in a safe action)

**General rules first:**
1. **One abnormal value is a question, not a diagnosis.** Repeat it in the same lab under the same conditions (fasting, morning, no hard workout for 48 hours, no acute illness) before acting [literature]. This is where his "time-lapse labs" idea is right [inferred].
2. **Check the unit before comparing to any target.** See §0 [inferred].
3. **No rule below changes a drug dose.** Any result on a medicine goes to the prescriber [boundary].

| # | If… | Then… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fasting insulin >10 µIU/mL, or HOMA-IR >2.5, with normal glucose | **Change foundations** (§3a below) for 12 weeks, then **retest** fasting glucose, insulin and HbA1c in the same lab. Share the results with the physician at the next visit. |
| 2 | Fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7–6.4% | **Retest** to confirm, **change foundations**, and **see the physician** for a prediabetes plan. Recheck yearly at minimum. |
| 3 | Fasting glucose ≥126 twice, or HbA1c ≥6.5% | **See the physician promptly.** This is diabetes until proven otherwise. |
| 4 | On any glucose-lowering medicine, with glucose <70 mg/dL or hypoglycemia symptoms (shaking, sweating, confusion) | Treat the low per the plan the clinician gave you. **If you have no plan:** take about 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate (glucose tablets or juice), recheck in 15 minutes, and repeat if still <70. If the person is confused, cannot swallow or is unconscious, give nothing by mouth and **call emergency services (101 in Israel)**. Then **contact the physician the same day**. **Do not adjust medication yourself.** |
| 5 | HbA1c and glucose/CGM disagree, or there is anemia, iron deficiency or thalassemia trait | **See the physician.** HbA1c may be unreliable; an alternative such as fructosamine or OGTT is a clinical choice. |
| 6 | hs-CRP >3 mg/L (check the unit) | **Retest in 2–3 weeks** when well. If still >3, **see the physician** to assess cardiovascular risk and look for hidden infection or inflammation. If >10, **see the physician** to find the cause. |
| 7 | hs-CRP 1–3 mg/L | **Change foundations** (sleep, waist, walking, strength, alcohol). **Retest in 3 months.** Interpret alongside ApoB and Lp(a), not instead of them. |
| 8 | ALT above ACG healthy limits (men >33, women >25) but ≤2× the lab ULN | Stop alcohol, review supplements and medicines **with the physician** (many "liver support" stacks and green-tea extracts are hepatotoxic), **change foundations**, and **retest in 8–12 weeks**. Ask for FIB-4. |
| 8b | ALT or AST >2× and up to 3× the lab ULN | **Retest within 2–4 weeks**, **stop non-prescribed supplements and compounds**, and **see the physician**. |
| 9 | ALT or AST >3× ULN, any jaundice, dark urine, or raised bilirubin with ALP | **Stop non-prescribed supplements and research compounds. See the physician the same week**, sooner if symptomatic. |
| 10 | FIB-4 ≥1.3 | **See the physician** and ask for elastography (FibroScan) per AASLD/EASL. |
| 11 | GGT raised in isolation | Review alcohol and medications (antiepileptics and others raise GGT), **change foundations**, **retest in 8–12 weeks**. Persistent rise means **see the physician**. |
| 12 | TG/HDL >3 (mg/dL) or TG >150 | **Change foundations**: cut fructose and sugary drinks, reduce alcohol, walk after meals, lose waist. **Retest lipids in 8–12 weeks.** TG >500 means **see the physician promptly** (pancreatitis risk). |
| 13 | LDL or ApoB above the risk-tier goal, or Lp(a) ≥50 mg/dL | **See the physician** for a full risk calculation. Lp(a) is genetic: tell first-degree relatives and suggest they get tested. **Never treat "my CRP is low" as cancelling high ApoB or Lp(a).** |

| 17 | B12 200–300 pg/mL, or any B12 deficit on metformin | **Retest with MMA** (and homocysteine if available) and **see the physician**. Neurological symptoms mean seeing the physician promptly. |
| 18 | Homocysteine >15 µmol/L | **See the physician**: check B12, folate, kidney function and thyroid, and review medicines. **Retest** after the cause is addressed. |
| 19 | TSH outside the lab range | **Retest TSH with FT4 in 6–12 weeks** (not during acute illness; stop biotin 48–72 hours before). If confirmed, **see the physician**. **Never start or change thyroid hormone yourself to "optimize" FT3.** |
| 20 | Low-normal FT3 during dieting or GLP-1 weight loss, with normal TSH | Expected adaptation. **Change foundations** (adequate protein and energy, sleep, strength training, slower loss rate). **Retest after the weight-loss phase.** Raise symptoms with the physician. |
| 21 | TPO antibodies positive | **See the physician.** Retest TSH **yearly**, or before and during pregnancy as advised. |
| 22 | Morning total testosterone low on one test | **Retest on a second morning, fasting.** If still low, **see the physician** for LH, FSH, prolactin, SHBG and a cause workup. |
| 23 | On TRT and hematocrit >54% (or rising toward it) | **Contact the prescriber promptly.** This is the guideline threshold for holding therapy and evaluating. Do not donate blood or change the dose on your own initiative. |
| 24 | On TRT or a restart protocol, with total testosterone, estradiol, LH/FSH or prolactin outside range | **Retest, then see the prescribing physician.** Every hCG, SERM or AI decision belongs to the physician (his self-run thresholds are withheld here). |

| 28 | eGFR <60 once, or creatinine up | **Retest in 2–4 weeks** while well hydrated, with no hard workout or creatine for several days. Persistent means **see the physician** (consider eGFRcr-cys). |

| 31b | On a GLP-1/GIP medicine with lipase >3× ULN and no symptoms | **Contact the prescriber the same day.** Do not change the medicine yourself. |
| 32 | On a GLP-1 medicine with vomiting or diarrhea for >24–48 hours, low urine output or dizziness | **Keep sipping fluids or an oral rehydration solution** if you can keep them down. Skip the next dose only if the prescriber's sick-day plan says so, and **contact the physician the same day**. **Go to the emergency department** if you cannot keep fluids down, have very low urine output or feel faint. Check creatinine and electrolytes (risk of acute kidney injury). |
| 33 | IGF-1 above the age range (>2 SDS), with or without any GH-axis product | **Stop non-prescribed GH-axis compounds and see the physician** (acromegaly and malignancy-risk context; the tesamorelin label requires a physician review if persistently >3 SDS). |
| 34 | Any result was produced while using research peptides or unapproved compounds | **Disclose everything to the physician.** Interpretation depends on it, and stopping is the default safe action. |

### 3a. "Change foundations": the lifestyle levers named above (exact numbers allowed)
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours, consistent wake time ±30 minutes [literature].
- **Morning light:** 10–30 minutes of outdoor light within an hour of waking, without looking directly at the sun. He says "15–20 minutes of direct sunlight" on waking [his framing, verified]; there is no RCT for a specific number [inferred].
- **Steps / walking:** 7,000–10,000 steps per day (the mortality benefit plateaus at about 6,000–8,000 steps for adults ≥60 and about 8,000–10,000 for adults <60; Paluch 2022), plus a 10–15 minute walk after the largest meal [literature].
- **Strength training:** 2–3 sessions per week covering major muscle groups (WHO / ACSM) [literature].
- **Protein:**
 - 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day during weight loss or GLP-1 use, for healthy kidneys [literature].
 - In CKD, follow the nephrologist (KDIGO about 0.8 g/kg). Never raise protein with eGFR <60 without the nephrologist [literature].
 - Note: his "per pound" protein figures are extreme per the critic audit [critic], and his claim that KDIGO recommends 1 g per pound in CKD is false (§1I).
- **Eating window:** a 10–12 hour window is reasonable for most people [literature/inferred]. **No prolonged fasting for anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas without the physician** [boundary].
- **Fructose / sugary drinks:** aim for zero sugary drinks, and keep added sugar below 10% of energy, ideally below 5% (WHO) [literature].
- **Alcohol:** raises GGT and triglycerides in a dose-dependent way. Less is better, and none is best when GGT, TG or ALT are raised [literature].
- **Hydration:** drink to pale-yellow urine. On GLP-1 medicines thirst is blunted, so plan fluids [literature/inferred]. **No sodium "loading"** [boundary].

## 6. Hook lines for content (his verified wording + an honest turn)

All of these are labelled [his framing] for the quote and [literature] for the turn.

- "ALT is the smoke alarm" that goes off after the house is already burning. True for the lag. So ask for **FIB-4**, the free fire-inspection score your doctor can calculate today.

- "if GFR and cystatin C disagree trust cystatin C". KDIGO 2024 broadly agrees: use both together.
- "you can't use TSH". Then he uses "TSH above 4.5 or below 0.3" himself. TSH stays the first test; it just isn't the only one.
