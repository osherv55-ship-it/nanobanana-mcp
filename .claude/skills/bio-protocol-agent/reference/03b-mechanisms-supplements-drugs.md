# MECHANISM KNOWLEDGE BASE, Part B: supplements, prescription drugs he discusses, and lifestyle levers

> **Scope.** Part B covers the non-peptide layer of the Bachmeyer-style agent: (1) supplements, (2) prescription drugs and hormones he promotes or attacks, and (3) the lifestyle levers he prescribes. Part A covers peptides, research compounds and injectable NAD+, and this part does not repeat that material.
> **Provenance tags.** `[his framing]` = his position as captured in the research corpus. `[literature]` = independent evidence (trials, guidelines, labels). `[inferred]` = this KB's own synthesis, or a regulatory or availability point that could not be verified.
> **Quotes.** A phrase appears in "quotation marks" only after it was matched against the normalized raw transcript corpus (`corpus_raw_norm.txt`). Text that could not be matched is marked `[paraphrase]`. Some matches come from video descriptions or chapter text, and those are labelled. ASR artifacts are possible.
> **Evidence grades.** A = human RCTs or meta-analysis for this indication. B = small RCTs or surrogate endpoints. C = observational, or small and inconsistent trials. D = animal or in-vitro only. Additional labels: **A-null** = adequate human RCTs show *no benefit* for this indication (strong evidence of no effect, not weak evidence). **Expert opinion / no trials** = no human trials; only consensus or mechanistic reasoning. **Not recommended (guidelines)** = guideline bodies advise against this use. A range such as A/B means the grade was downgraded, and the entry states the reason.

## 0. Operating rules for this part (non-negotiable)

1. **Dose permission by class.**
 - **Supplements:** an evidence-based dose is allowed, but only (a) as used in human trials and (b) within the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) or Observed Safe Level (OSL). Every supplement entry carries a grade.
 - **Prescription drugs and hormones:** no doses, routes, frequencies, cycles or titration rules. This holds even when attributed to him. Each drug entry ends with **"A decision for a physician."**
 - **Lifestyle:** exact numbers are allowed, nothing extreme.
2. **His supplement amounts that exceed a UL are not reproduced.** The KB only flags that they exceed it. This applies to magnesium, zinc, vitamin D, potassium, sodium and selenium in various videos `[literature]`/`[inferred]`.
3. **If/then rules always end in a safe action:** stop, retest, or see the physician. There are no self-titration rules for any drug, including "reduce your metformin or insulin as your CGM improves", which he does teach `[his framing]`. That rule is replaced with: *"glucose trending down while on diabetes medication → tell the prescribing physician now (hypoglycemia risk)."*
4. **Banned outputs:** "fire your doctor", stopping prescribed medication, cure promises ("100% reversible"), attacks on psychiatry or oncology, invented studies or percentages. His study citations with percentages are mostly unverifiable (see section B8), and none of them is repeated as fact.
5. **The Big-Three tag on every entry:** **INF** = systemic inflammation ("the fire"), **IR** = insulin resistance ("the fuel problem"), **ATP** = mitochondrial / ATP shortage ("the power outage"). The tag shows where *he* routes the item. The evidence line shows whether that routing holds in humans.

## B1. Core supplements (full entries)

### S1. Creatine monohydrate
- **Class:** nitrogenous organic acid; dietary supplement.
- **Big-Three:** ATP.
- **His framing:** He routes creatine to kidney tubular energy in CKD: supplemental creatine "spares these amino acids", raises creatine-kinase activity, and "your tubular cells need this" (verified, kidney video). He cites a "2016 Kidney International" study saying creatine *improves kidney function* in CKD; this citation is unverified. Elsewhere creatine appears only in a supplement list `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: creatine kinase + phosphocreatine (PCr) act as an **ATP buffer and shuttle**. In a fast effort, PCr hands its phosphate back to ADP in milliseconds, like a phone power bank that recharges the battery before it dies. It also moves high-energy phosphate from mitochondria to where ATP is used (myofibrils, ion pumps). Brain neurons use the same system, which is the basis for the memory effects.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for high-intensity performance and training adaptation (decades of RCTs; ISSN position stand).
 - Grade B for memory in older adults: 2023 meta-analysis of 8 RCTs, SMD 0.88 in ages 66–76 vs about 0 in young adults. A 2024 meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (n=492) found effects on memory but not on executive function `[literature]`.
 - **Expert opinion / no trials: no human trial supports "improves kidney function in CKD"; the claim is unsupported.** Creatine raises *serum creatinine* through normal conversion, which can falsely lower eGFR. That makes a lab problem, not a kidney benefit `[literature]`.
- **Dose (trials, within safety data):** 3–5 g/day creatine monohydrate. An optional loading phase uses ~0.3 g/kg/day (about 20 g/day in 4 split doses) for 5–7 days; it is not needed, since 3 g/day reaches saturation in ~28 days. Long-term safety is described in healthy people up to 30 g/day for 5 years, but there is no reason to exceed 5 g/day `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Water-weight gain of 0.5–1 kg in the first weeks.
 - GI upset if taken in large single doses.
 - **CKD or eGFR <60, a single kidney, or nephrotoxic drugs → nephrologist decision first.**
 - Tell the lab or physician you take creatine; if needed, ask for **cystatin C** to estimate GFR independently of creatinine.
 - A small youth-athlete RCT suggested airway effects in atopic athletes.
 - Not WADA-prohibited `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** sold freely as a sports or food supplement (pharmacies, sports shops, import). Registry status not verified `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** קריאטין = "סוללת גיבוי" ל-ATP. ראיות A לביצועים, B לזיכרון במבוגרים. 3–5 גרם ביום. מעלה קריאטינין במעבדה, אז לבקש ציסטטין C, ובמחלת כליות רק באישור נפרולוג.

### S2. Magnesium (forms: glycinate/bisglycinate, citrate, L-threonate, oxide)
- **Class:** essential mineral.
- **Big-Three:** ATP (primary), IR, INF.
- **His framing:**
 - "atp is useless without magnesium" (verified).
 - On oxide: "it's a laxative not a nutrient" (verified).
 - Glycinate is his default. He says the glycine carrier is itself calming, and pairs magnesium with L-theanine as "your cortisol management system" (verified).
 - He claims threonate is the only form that crosses the blood–brain barrier, citing Slutsky 2010 *Neuron* (rat) `[his framing]`.
 - He asks for RBC magnesium rather than serum `[his framing]`.
 - His daily amounts exceed the supplemental UL (number not reproduced).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - Inside cells, ATP exists mostly as **Mg-ATP**. Magnesium is the "hand" that holds the ATP so enzymes can grab it, so "useless without magnesium" is a fair simplification for many ATP-dependent enzymes.
 - It is a cofactor for hundreds of enzymes (glycolysis, DNA/protein synthesis, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase).
 - It is a physiological blocker of the NMDA receptor (a cork in the glutamate channel) and a calcium-channel modulator. Those two actions are the neural and vascular rationale.
 - About 50–60% of body magnesium sits in bone. **Serum Mg is a poor index of total stores**, which is why he wants RBC Mg. That is reasonable, but RBC Mg is not standardized either.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for correcting deficiency.
 - Grade B for blood pressure: 34 RCTs, median 368 mg/day, SBP −2.0 / DBP −1.8 mmHg.
 - Grade B for glucose and insulin sensitivity markers in deficient or high-risk people.
 - Grade C for sleep: 3 small RCTs in older insomniacs, about −17 min sleep latency, high risk of bias.
 - Grade C/D for threonate and cognition: the rodent data are real; human data are small.
 - **Form differences:** organic salts (citrate, glycinate, chloride) are absorbed better than oxide. The precise percentages he quotes (~30% vs 4–5%) are not verified `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 200–350 mg/day *elemental* magnesium from supplements. **US UL for supplemental Mg is 350 mg/day**; EFSA's is 250 mg/day; food magnesium is not capped. Split into 2 doses if GI-sensitive. RDA is 400–420 mg (men) and 310–320 mg (women) total `[literature]`. Threonate products: trial-used amounts deliver ~100–150 mg elemental Mg/day; count that toward the same cap `[inferred]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Diarrhea is the first sign of excess (oxide and citrate most).
 - **Kidney disease (eGFR <30, or any CKD without physician input) → risk of hypermagnesemia; physician decision.**
 - Separate by ≥2 h from bisphosphonates, tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics, levothyroxine and iron.
 - Long-term PPIs cause low Mg (a reason to test).
- **Israel:** all forms are widely sold. Serum Mg is routine through kupot. RBC Mg is generally not a routine kupah test (not verified) `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** מגנזיום מחזיק את ה-ATP ("יד שמחזיקה את המטבע"). ראיות A לחוסר, B ללחץ דם וסוכר. עד 350 מ"ג ביום מתוסף (יסודי). במחלת כליות רק באישור רופא.

### S3. Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)
- **Class:** long-chain n-3 fatty acids.
- **Big-Three:** INF.
- **His framing:**
 - "i take a ton of omega 3" (verified).
 - For anxiety he claims it was "as effective as fluoxetine… for anxiety but with no side effects" (verified as his words; the citation he gives, "Zanarini", is unverified, see section B8).
 - In the dementia video: DHA as neuronal membrane structure, plus restoring the omega-3:omega-6 ratio "wrecked" by seed oils `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - EPA and DHA build into cell membranes. They change membrane fluidity and signaling "rafts", and compete with arachidonic acid, so the eicosanoids made are less inflammatory.
 - They are raw material for **specialized pro-resolving mediators** (resolvins, protectins, maresins). These are the "clean-up crew" that actively switches inflammation *off*, rather than suppressing its start. That is the closest real match to his "resolve, not suppress" theme.
 - Triglyceride lowering comes from reduced liver VLDL output and more β-oxidation.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for CV outcomes **only for high-dose purified EPA (icosapent ethyl) in statin-treated people with high triglycerides** (REDUCE-IT, a large positive RCT), with a downgrade caveat: REDUCE-IT's mineral-oil comparator may have worsened outcomes in the placebo arm, and STRENGTH (EPA+DHA) was null.
 - A-null for general primary prevention: VITAL (1 g/day) was null, and Cochrane 2018 was null for general populations.
 - Grade A for lowering triglycerides at 2–4 g/day.
 - Grade C for anxiety (a 2018 meta-analysis suggests benefit at ≥2 g/day; small trials).
 - **The "as effective as fluoxetine, no side effects" claim is not supported as stated** `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** general use 1–2 g/day EPA+DHA with meals. EFSA: up to 5 g/day combined EPA+DHA raises no safety concern. **Doses ≥2–4 g/day for triglycerides belong under physician supervision** (prescription EPA exists, and AF risk rises with dose: a meta-analysis found higher AF risk, steeper above ~1 g/day) `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Atrial fibrillation (REDUCE-IT AF hospitalization 3.1% vs 2.1%).
 - Additive bleeding risk with anticoagulants, antiplatelets or high-dose curcumin/vitamin E. Tell the surgeon or dentist.
 - GI upset and fishy reflux.
 - Fish allergy (use algae oil).
- **Israel:** widely sold; prescription purified-EPA availability not verified. The omega-3 index is not a routine kupah test (private dried-blood-spot kits exist; not verified) `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** אומגה 3 = חומר גלם ל"צוות הכיבוי" שמסיים דלקת. 1–2 גרם ביום. 4 גרם רק לטריגליצרידים גבוהים ובליווי רופא (סיכון לפרפור פרוזדורים). זהירות עם מדללי דם.

### S4. Vitamin D3 (+ K2 framing)
- **Class:** secosteroid prohormone (D3); vitamin K2 menaquinone-7 (MK-7).
- **Big-Three:** INF (immune), IR.
- **His framing:**
 - "vitamin d is a steroid hormone precursor" (verified).
 - He pairs it with K2: "d3 and k2 you can't take one without the other they're married" (verified).
 - K2 activates osteocalcin to put calcium "in bone, not arteries" `[paraphrase]`.
 - He warns that one "can take far too many handfuls of vitamin d3" (verified), yet in other videos states a daily D3 amount above the UL (not reproduced).
 - His lab target is above the IOM sufficiency band `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: D3 → liver → 25(OH)D (the storage form you measure) → kidney and tissues → **1,25(OH)₂D**. That binds the vitamin D receptor, a **nuclear transcription factor** (a "master switch" on DNA) controlling gut calcium absorption, bone remodeling and many immune genes. K2 is the cofactor that **carboxylates** (activates) osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein. Think of K2 as the "traffic cop" that tags calcium-binding proteins so calcium goes to bone and is kept out of artery walls.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for correcting deficiency and for bone and mineral health.
 - **A-null for preventing cancer, CVD or mortality in people who are already replete.** VITAL (2,000 IU/day, n=25,871), a large RCT, was null for cancer incidence, MACE and mortality, with a suggestive signal only for cancer *mortality*. This is strong evidence of no benefit, not weak evidence.
 - K2: grade C. MK-7 180 mcg/day for 3 years reduced arterial stiffness in postmenopausal women (one RCT). The larger AVADEC trial (MK-7 + D3, 2 years) did not significantly slow coronary calcification progression. "Married" is a defensible pairing for high-dose D users, not a proven necessity `[literature]`.
- **Dose:**
 - D3: 1,000–2,000 IU/day maintenance; **UL 4,000 IU/day** (IOM and EFSA 100 µg). Correcting documented deficiency above the UL is a **physician-supervised** decision.
 - Target 25(OH)D: IOM sufficiency 20–50 ng/mL; Endocrine Society 30–100. Aiming for 30–50 ng/mL is a reasonable middle `[literature]`/`[inferred]`.
 - K2 (MK-7): 90–180 mcg/day as in trials; no UL set.
 - Take D with a fat-containing meal.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Hypercalcemia, kidney stones and soft-tissue calcification with chronic excess (levels above ~150 ng/mL).
 - Caution with sarcoidosis, granulomatous disease, hyperparathyroidism, thiazides.
 - **K2 directly antagonizes warfarin and other vitamin K antagonists → never start K2 on warfarin without the anticoagulation clinic.** It does not interact with DOACs the same way `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** D3 is widely sold. **Kupot do not test vitamin D routinely; testing requires a clinical indication** (Maccabi eligibility page) `[literature]`. Deficiency is common in Israel despite sun, especially in women who cover their skin.
- **HE:** ויטמין D הוא מתג גנטי (קולטן גרעיני). ראיות A לחוסר ולעצם. למניעת סרטן ולב כשהרמה תקינה: A-null (ניסויים גדולים לא הראו תועלת). 1,000–2,000 IU ביום, עד 4,000. K2 אסור עם קומדין בלי רופא.

### S5. Taurine
- **Class:** amino sulfonic acid.
- **Big-Three:** IR, ATP (his routing: calming / INF).
- **His framing:**
 - "taurine is one of my favorite things you can take… never mind just menopause" (verified).
 - He calls it a GABA-A / glycine-receptor agonist ("GABA mimetic") that calms over-firing hypothalamic neurons and night sweats.
 - He says oral GABA "doesn't cross the blood brain barrier" (taurine does).
 - He also claims stimulant-heavy fat-loss regimens deplete taurine `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - It sits at ~20 mM inside heart and muscle and is moved by the TauT (SLC6A6) transporter.
 - Jobs: osmotic balance, calcium handling, bile-acid conjugation, antioxidant buffering.
 - Mitochondrially, it **modifies mitochondrial tRNA**. That is "spell-check" for the factory's own protein-making, so without it complex I/III subunits are mistranslated.
 - It is a weak agonist at glycine and GABA-A receptors (the "brake pedal" story is mechanistically real, magnitude uncertain in humans).
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade B for cardiometabolic surrogates: a 2026 meta-analysis of 34 RCTs found fasting glucose −5.9 mg/dL, HbA1c −0.21%, HOMA-IR −0.57, TG −14 mg/dL, SBP −4.4 mmHg, plus lower CRP.
 - **Grade D for longevity:** the 2023 *Science* lifespan data are mouse/worm/monkey only.
 - Grade C/D for anxiety and hot flashes in humans.
 - Counterweight: a 2025 mouse study found taurine promoted lung-tumor growth (biphasic). Not a reason to avoid it, but a reason not to oversell it `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 1.5–3 g/day (the RCT "sweet spot"). Observed Safe Level 3 g/day. Blood-pressure effects show by <8 weeks; glucose/lipid effects need ≥8 weeks `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Very well tolerated. Theoretical additive effects with antihypertensives or lithium (taurine may affect lithium clearance; check with prescriber) `[inferred]`.
 - **People with active or treated myeloid leukemia: oncologist decision.** 2025 *Nature* preclinical data (Sharma et al.) showed leukemia cells take up taurine via the same TauT/SLC6A6 transporter to fuel growth `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** sold as a supplement `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** טאורין = "בודק איות" לחלבוני המיטוכונדריה וגם בלם עצבי עדין. ראיות B לסוכר, שומנים ולחץ דם. D לאריכות ימים. 1.5–3 גרם ביום. בלוקמיה מיאלואידית (פעילה או לאחר טיפול) רק באישור אונקולוג.

### S6. Glycine / GlyNAC
- **Class:** non-essential amino acid; GlyNAC = glycine + N-acetylcysteine.
- **Big-Three:** INF (glutathione), ATP.
- **His framing:** glycine acts on spinal/brainstem glycine receptors, an "inhibitory signal" lowering pain and hypervigilance, and is an NMDA co-agonist that boosts other anxiolytics. He chooses magnesium and zinc *glycinate* partly for the glycine `[his framing]`/`[paraphrase]`. GlyNAC as a named combination was not found in his own words.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - Glutathione (GSH) is built from 3 bricks: glutamate, cysteine, glycine. In aging, cysteine and glycine run short, so the "body's main antioxidant" is under-built.
 - Glycine also activates inhibitory glycine receptors and is an NMDA co-agonist.
 - Pre-sleep glycine causes peripheral vasodilation, which drops core temperature and is the proposed sleep-onset mechanism.
- **Evidence:**
 - GlyNAC grade C. A 16-week RCT (n=24, from the originating group) reported broad improvements. A larger independent RCT (n=114, 2 weeks) **missed its primary endpoint** (no rise in GSH or the GSH:GSSG ratio).
 - Glycine for sleep: grade C (small Japanese trials, e.g., Inagawa 2006 and Yamadera 2007; ~3 g at bedtime; subjective sleep quality) `[literature, not independently verified in sheet]`.
- **Dose:**
 - Glycine 3 g ~30–60 min before bed (as in small trials) `[literature, not independently verified in sheet]`.
 - GlyNAC for general use: 2.4 g/day total in a 1:1 glycine:NAC ratio (about 1.2 g NAC), the low dose from the independent trial. That trial lasted only 2 weeks; longer-term safety data are limited `[literature]`. **Do not combine with separate NAC; GlyNAC counts toward the NAC total in S7.**
- **Risks / interactions:** mild GI upset. Theoretical interaction with clozapine (glycine-site NMDA modulation; clozapine efficacy was reduced in one study), so a psychiatrist must decide if you take clozapine `[literature]`. NAC cautions as in S7.
- **Israel:** glycine is sold; GlyNAC products are mostly imported `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** גליצין = לבנה לגלוטתיון ובלם עצבי. 3 גרם לפני שינה (ראיות C). ל-GlyNAC הניסוי הגדול והבלתי תלוי היה שלילי. לא לשלב עם NAC נפרד.

### S7. NAC (N-acetylcysteine)
- **Class:** cysteine prodrug; also a licensed mucolytic and the paracetamol antidote.
- **Big-Three:** INF.
- **His framing:**
 - In dementia: replenishes cysteine for glutathione and lowers neuroinflammation; injectable glutathione is presented as bypassing the need for NAC `[his framing]`.
 - For liver stress during rapid fat loss: NAC rather than oral glutathione, because "just taking glutathione won't help" (verified).
 - In one fructose video he lumps "milk thistle, NAC, silymarin" as "marginal benefit at best" `[paraphrase]`, so his position is internally inconsistent.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: NAC delivers **cysteine, the rate-limiting brick** for glutathione synthesis, and also breaks disulfide bonds in mucus. Glutamate modulation via the cystine–glutamate antiporter is the basis for psychiatric research. Metaphor: glutathione is the cell's "fire extinguisher"; NAC refills the extinguisher's scarcest chemical.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A: paracetamol overdose (as a drug).
 - Grade A/B: fewer COPD exacerbations at 1,200 mg/day (PANTHEON, a large RCT; downgraded for generalizability because the population was Chinese).
 - Grade C: adjunct in some psychiatric conditions (trichotillomania, cannabis-use disorder, bipolar depression, all mixed).
 - Grade D/C for "neuroinflammation in dementia" `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 600–1,200 mg/day in 1–2 doses (as in trials) `[literature]`. Any NAC from GlyNAC (S6) counts toward this total.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - GI upset; sulfur odor; rare bronchospasm (mostly the inhaled form).
 - **Nitroglycerin: additive hypotension and headache.**
 - Activated charcoal reduces absorption.
 - Theoretical blunting of training adaptation from antioxidants (avoid right around workouts if training adaptation is the goal) `[inferred]`.
 - US status: FDA has said it will exercise enforcement discretion on NAC supplements `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** NAC is sold as an OTC mucolytic medicine (e.g., effervescent acetylcysteine); exact registry status not verified `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** NAC ממלא מחדש את "מטף הכיבוי" (גלוטתיון). ראיות A/B ל-COPD. 600–1,200 מ"ג ביום (כולל ה-NAC שב-GlyNAC). זהירות עם ניטרוגליצרין.

### S8. PEA (palmitoylethanolamide)
- **Class:** endogenous fatty-acid amide.
- **Big-Three:** INF.
- **His framing:** named in his supporting cast for **pain** `[his framing]`, with no mechanism given in captured material.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - It activates the nuclear receptor **PPAR-α**, a "dimmer switch" on inflammatory genes.
 - It has an "entourage effect": it competes for the enzymes (FAAH/NAAA) that degrade endocannabinoids, so the body's own anandamide lasts longer.
 - It calms **mast cells and glia** (the "ALIA" concept: local inflammation antagonism).
- **Evidence:** grade B, with a bias caveat. A meta-analysis of 11 double-blind RCTs (n=774) reduced pain, but the effect size (SMD 1.68) is implausibly large for an analgesic, which suggests bias. Much of the literature comes from few groups and manufacturers. No major adverse events reported `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 600–1,200 mg/day (micronized or ultramicronized), usually in 2 doses, for ≥4–8 weeks. Trials also used 300 mg acutely (menstrual pain) `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:** well tolerated; few known interactions. Pregnancy data are limited, so avoid `[literature]`/`[inferred]`.
- **Israel:** sold as a food supplement (EU-style), not verified `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** PEA מעמעם דלקת דרך PPAR-α ותאי פיטום. ראיות B לכאב כרוני, עם הסתייגות (גודל אפקט חשוד). 600–1,200 מ"ג ביום.

### S9. Curcumin
- **Class:** polyphenol (*Curcuma longa*).
- **Big-Three:** INF.
- **His framing:** dismissive. In passing he says (roughly) keep taking your curcumin thinking that's going to do it, "it's more than that" `[paraphrase]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: inhibits NF-κB (the inflammation "master switch"), modulates COX/LOX, activates Nrf2 (the antioxidant gene program). Caveat: curcumin is a **PAINS compound**, a "false positive" in lab assays, so many of its reported targets are artifacts. Its core problem is bioavailability (poor absorption, rapid glucuronidation), which piperine or phospholipid/liposomal forms partly fix.

- **Dose:** 500–1,500 mg/day curcumin or standardized extract, for ≥12 weeks for OA (trial range 120–1,500 mg). Context: EFSA's food-additive ADI is 3 mg/kg/day; trial supplement doses exceed it, which is one reason for the liver caution below `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - **Hepatotoxicity:** rare but documented cluster with high-bioavailability or piperine formulations → stop and check LFTs if there is fatigue, dark urine or jaundice.
 - GI upset; gallstone/biliary obstruction caution.
 - Additive antiplatelet effect (anticoagulants).
 - Piperine raises levels of many drugs (CYP inhibition).
- **Israel:** widely sold `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** כורכומין מנמיך את מתג הדלקת NF-κB. ראיות B לכאבי ברכיים (שחיקה). 500–1,500 מ"ג ביום. לפורמולות עם פיפרין יש דיווחי פגיעה בכבד, לעקוב אחרי אנזימי כבד.

### S11. Berberine
- **Class:** isoquinoline alkaloid.
- **Big-Three:** IR.
- **His framing:** named for "insulin sensitivity" in his supporting cast `[his framing]`. No extended discussion captured.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - Activates **AMPK** (the cell's "low-fuel warning light"), so more fat burning and less fat building.
 - Modulates gut microbiota and endotoxemia.
 - Oral bioavailability is **<1%** (P-gp pumps it back out), so much of its action likely happens *in the gut* and through metabolites.
- **Evidence:** grade B for surrogates. A 46-trial meta-analysis found HbA1c −0.73% and FPG −0.86 mmol/L, plus improvements in lipids and HOMA-IR. Effects attenuate after 90 days, above 2 g/day, and in people over 60. Trials are mainly Chinese, with variable quality and no hard outcomes `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 500 mg 2–3×/day with meals (0.9–1.5 g/day as in trials); no benefit above 2 g/day `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions (gate before use):**
 - **Inhibits CYP2D6, CYP3A4, CYP2C9 and P-gp.** It interacts with cyclosporine, tacrolimus, many statins, some anticoagulants, metformin and others → **anyone on prescription drugs: pharmacist or physician check first.**
 - **Additive hypoglycemia with diabetes drugs** → physician.
 - **Contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding** (neonatal kernicterus concern).
 - GI effects (constipation, diarrhea).
 - Do not frame it as "natural metformin" `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** sold mainly via import `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** ברברין מדליק את "נורת הדלק" AMPK, בעיקר במעי. ראיות B ל-HbA1c. 500 מ"ג פעמיים-שלוש ביום. הרבה אינטראקציות (CYP/P-gp), אסור בהריון, ועם תרופות לסוכרת רק דרך רופא.

### S12. Zinc (+ copper balance)
- **Class:** essential trace mineral.
- **Big-Three:** INF (immune), IR (insulin storage and signaling).
- **His framing:**
 - "zinc is the anabolic mineral period" (verified).
 - Required for the first step of steroidogenesis; claims deficiency caps testosterone "at about 50%" (unverified).
 - Warns that zinc without copper causes "copper deficiency… a disaster" (verified), and that ~40 mg+ breaks the zinc:copper ratio `[his framing]`.
 - In other videos his daily zinc exceeds the UL (not reproduced).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: >3,000 proteins use zinc (zinc-finger transcription factors are "clamps" that hold DNA-reading proteins in shape). It is needed for immune-cell development, insulin crystal storage in β-cells, and testosterone synthesis *when deficient*. High zinc induces gut metallothionein, which traps copper, so chronic excess causes **copper-deficiency anemia, neutropenia and myelopathy**.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for correcting deficiency.
 - Grade C for colds (Cochrane 2024: −2.4 days, low certainty, I²=97%, more side effects).
 - Grade C for raising testosterone (only in deficient men) `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 8–11 mg/day RDA; 15–25 mg/day supplemental is reasonable when intake is low. **UL 40 mg/day from all sources** (EFSA 25 mg). If ≥25 mg/day long-term, add copper 1–2 mg/day (copper UL 10 mg). Cold-lozenge trials used amounts above the UL. This KB does not recommend exceeding 40 mg/day total; discuss any short course with a physician or pharmacist `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Nausea on an empty stomach.
 - Separate by ≥2 h from quinolones and tetracyclines.
 - Reduces absorption of penicillamine and some antiretrovirals.
 - **Never intranasal zinc (anosmia).**
- **Israel:** widely sold `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** אבץ = מהדק לחלבונים. ראיות A לחוסר. 15–25 מ"ג ביום, תקרה 40. מעל 25 לאורך זמן להוסיף 1–2 מ"ג נחושת.

### S13. CoQ10 / ubiquinol
- **Class:** lipid-soluble electron carrier.
- **Big-Three:** ATP.
- **His framing:** listed as electron-transport-chain support during mitochondrial adaptation, and as a nutrient that statins deplete ("CoQ10 loss impairs mitochondrial function", from the kidney video) `[paraphrase]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: CoQ10 is the **ferry boat** carrying electrons from complexes I and II to complex III in the inner mitochondrial membrane, and a membrane antioxidant in its reduced form (ubiquinol). Statins block the mevalonate pathway upstream of CoQ10 synthesis, so lower plasma CoQ10 on statins is real.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade B as an adjunct in HFrEF: Q-SYMBIO (n=420, 2 years) found fewer major CV events. It is one trial, so it needs replication.
 - Grade C for statin-associated muscle symptoms (meta-analyses conflict).
 - Grade C for migraine prophylaxis `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 100–300 mg/day with fat (300 mg in Q-SYMBIO). Observed Safe Level 1,200 mg/day. Ubiquinol vs ubiquinone: modest absorption differences, with no outcome superiority shown `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:** GI upset, insomnia if taken late. **It may reduce the effect of warfarin (INR drop), so check with the anticoagulation clinic.** Possible additive BP lowering `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** widely sold `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** CoQ10 = "מעבורת" אלקטרונים במיטוכונדריה. ראיות B כתוסף באי ספיקת לב, C לכאבי שרירים מסטטינים. 100–300 מ"ג. לבדוק אם נוטלים קומדין.

### S14. NMN / NR (oral NAD+ precursors)
- **Class:** vitamin B3-family nucleotides / nucleosides.
- **Big-Three:** ATP.
- **His framing:**
 - "i'm not a huge fan of precursors" (verified); they "get degraded pretty hard no matter what."
 - Video description: "oral nad supplements are a scam" (verified).
 - He promotes injectable NAD+ instead (covered in Part A, no dosing).
 - Inconsistently, his store sold an "NMN" product inside a stack `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: NAD+ is the **electron "shuttle bus"** (NAD+ ⇄ NADH) feeding complex I, and the fuel that sirtuins, PARPs and CD38 burn. NR and NMN enter the salvage pathway (NMN is often converted to NR at the cell surface, then rebuilt). **Oral precursors reliably raise blood NAD+**, which contradicts "oral doesn't work". The open question is whether that changes outcomes. Note `[literature]`: injected NAD+ is also largely broken down outside cells by CD38/CD73 into precursors, which undercuts the "injection is 100× better" claim.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade B for raising NAD+ (multiple RCTs; dose-ranging NMN 600–900 mg gave the highest levels).
 - Grade C for clinical outcomes. NMN 250 mg/day for 10 weeks improved muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic postmenopausal women (*Science* 2021, n=25). NR 1 g/day changed nothing in insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial function or liver fat in obese men, and nothing in MCI cognition or CKD exercise capacity `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** NMN 250–900 mg/day; NR 300–1,000 mg/day (trial ranges, well tolerated). The niacin UL (35 mg) applies to nicotinic acid and nicotinamide, not to NR or NMN `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:** mild GI upset and flushing are rare. Theoretical concern: NAD+ fuels all cells, including cancer cells, so people with active cancer should get an oncologist decision `[inferred]`.
- **Regulatory:** **US FDA (letters of Sept 29, 2025) reversed its 2022 position and confirmed NMN is lawful in dietary supplements**, still subject to NDI notification `[literature]`. Israel status not verified.
- **HE:** NMN/NR מעלים NAD+ בדם (ראיות B), אבל התועלת הקלינית לא הוכחה (C). NMN 250–900 מ"ג, NR 300–1,000 מ"ג. הטענה ש"דרך הפה לא עובד" לא נכונה לגבי הסמן.

### S15. L-carnitine (and acetyl-L-carnitine)
- **Class:** conditionally essential amine.
- **Big-Three:** ATP, IR.
- **His framing:** "this is the shuttle" (verified). Fatty acids cannot enter mitochondria without CPT-1 and carnitine, so during rapid fat mobilization "carnitine becomes the rate-limiting nutrient" (verified). In the same talk he concedes you "can't just take more carnitine and nad" to lift the ceiling (verified fragment: "you can't just take more carnitine and nad and fad").
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: long-chain fatty acids are the "trucks" that cannot pass the mitochondrial gate on their own. Carnitine is the "pass" swapped onto them by CPT-1 and swapped off by CPT-2 inside. In healthy omnivores muscle carnitine is rarely limiting, and oral carnitine raises muscle carnitine only slowly (and needs insulin).
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade C. Modest weight loss in meta-analyses (~1 kg).
 - Small NAFLD/insulin-sensitivity trials with mixed results.
 - Acetyl-L-carnitine: grade C for diabetic neuropathy pain and mild cognitive symptoms `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 1–2 g/day L-carnitine (Observed Safe Level 2 g/day); ALCAR 1–2 g/day in trials `[literature]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - **Raises TMAO** (gut bacteria convert it). The CV relevance is debated, so it is a reason not to exceed 2 g/day.
 - **Acts as a peripheral thyroid-hormone antagonist** (used experimentally in hyperthyroidism) → people on levothyroxine or with hypothyroidism: physician check.
 - Fishy body odor; GI upset.
 - Caution with seizure history (case reports) `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** sold `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** קרניטין = "האישור" שמכניס חומצות שומן למיטוכונדריה. ראיות C. עד 2 גרם ביום. מעלה TMAO ומפריע להורמון התריס.

### S16. TUDCA (tauroursodeoxycholic acid), evidence flag
- **Class:** taurine-conjugated bile acid. Its parent, **UDCA (ursodiol), is a prescription drug.**
- **Big-Three:** IR (liver).
- **His framing:** on GLP-1/GIP/glucagon fat-loss regimens, TUDCA is "the most critical thing you can do for preventing the biliary sludge and cholestasis… and nobody has ever talked about it". "it's not a supplement it's prevention" (both verified). It "makes bile wetter" and keeps cholesterol soluble `[his framing]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: hydrophilic bile acids dilute "detergent-harsh" hydrophobic bile acids, lower cholesterol saturation of bile, and act as **chemical chaperones** that reduce ER stress in liver cells. The ER-stress → insulin-signaling link is real in models. Rapid weight loss and GLP-1-class drugs do raise gallbladder and biliary events (meta-analysis RR ~1.4).
- **Evidence — read carefully:**
 - **UDCA (prescription)** has grade A evidence for preventing gallstones after **bariatric** rapid weight loss.
 - **TUDCA (supplement)** has **no trial for gallstone prevention on GLP-1-class drugs.**
 - TUDCA 1.75 g/day for 4 weeks improved hepatic and muscle insulin sensitivity in a small obese cohort (n≈20, grade C).
 - The phase 3 TUDCA-ALS trial did not meet its primary endpoint `[literature]`.
 - Overall: **grade C/D for his indication.** "The most critical thing" is unsupported.
- **Dose:** trial use was ~1–1.75 g/day for weeks only. **Gallstone prevention during rapid weight loss is a physician decision (UDCA is the evidenced option)**, not a supplement substitution `[literature]`/`[inferred]`.
- **Risks / interactions:**
 - Diarrhea.
 - **Right-upper-quadrant pain, fever, jaundice, or pain after fatty meals = urgent medical assessment** (gallstones/cholecystitis), not more supplements.
 - Bile-acid sequestrants block it.
 - Avoid in biliary obstruction `[literature]`.
- **Israel:** UDCA is available by prescription. TUDCA is mostly imported; status not verified `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** TUDCA "מרכך מרה". אין ניסוי למניעת אבני מרה תחת תרופות ירידה במשקל (C/D). את UDCA במרשם רופא קובע. כאב בצד ימין עליון, חום או צהבת = לרופא מיד.

### S17. Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium)
- **Class:** minerals.
- **Big-Three:** ATP (membrane potential, Na⁺/K⁺ pump).
- **His framing:**
 - On cutting carbs: "your kidneys dump sodium and water like they're trying to empty a sinking ship"; without electrolytes "you will get what they call keto flu your blood pressure will tank" (verified).
 - "stop drinking regular water… it screws with your blood plasma" (verified).
 - "sip don't chug" (verified).
 - On hypertension: "it's not a sodium problem it's an insulin problem" (verified).
 - He recommends potassium "food-first" (supplements need a prescription above OTC strength) `[his framing]`. In other videos he gives potassium and sodium amounts far above guidelines (not reproduced).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - Insulin tells the kidney tubule to **retain sodium**. When insulin falls (carb restriction, GLP-1-class drugs), there is natriuresis plus glycogen-bound water loss, which can cause dizziness and orthostatic hypotension. This part of his story is correct.
 - Hyperinsulinemia does promote sodium retention, so **both** insulin and sodium intake affect BP. It is not either/or.
 - Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase consumes a large share of resting ATP to keep the ~−70 mV "charged battery" of nerve and muscle cells.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A that sodium reduction lowers BP (DASH-Sodium; SSaSS: a salt substitute reduced stroke and death).
 - Grade A that higher dietary potassium lowers BP.
 - Grade C for relief of keto-flu symptoms with added electrolytes (clinical reports; no outcome RCTs).
 - **His "drink no plain water" and multi-gram added-sodium advice contradicts guidelines and is dangerous in CKD, heart failure, hypertension, or on diuretics/RAAS drugs** `[literature]`.
- **Dose (guideline-bounded):**
 - **Sodium:** ≤2,300 mg/day general adults (WHO <2,000 mg). Exception: during the **first 1–2 weeks** of strict carb restriction *with normal BP and no heart/kidney disease*, adding ~1 g sodium/day (e.g., salted broth) for symptoms is common clinical practice (expert opinion, no trials). **Not for anyone with hypertension, heart failure or CKD, or taking diuretics or RAAS drugs (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone). Stop if BP rises or ankles swell.**
 - **Potassium:** food-first, adequate intake 3,400 mg (men) / 2,600 mg (women) total. Potassium pills only as prescribed.
 - **Magnesium:** see S2.
- **Risks:**
 - **Potassium supplements + CKD / ACE inhibitor / ARB / spironolactone / trimethoprim → hyperkalemia (arrhythmia).**
 - Excess sodium → higher BP, fluid retention.
 - Over-drinking plain water without salt during long endurance events → hyponatremia.
- **Israel:** salt substitutes and oral-rehydration products are sold. Electrolytes are routine labs through kupot `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** כשאינסולין יורד, הכליה מאבדת נתרן ומים (נכון). אבל נתרן כן מעלה לחץ דם. עד 2,300 מ"ג נתרן ביום. אשלגן מהמזון. תוסף אשלגן רק עם מרשם, ובמחלת כליות או תרופות ללחץ דם אף פעם לבד.

### S18. Protein / leucine / EAA
- **Class:** macronutrient; leucine = branched-chain amino acid; EAA = essential amino acids.
- **Big-Three:** IR (muscle as glucose sink), ATP (mitochondrial protein turnover).
- **His framing:**
 - "leucine is designed to wake up mtor" (verified).
 - Protein must come from animal sources: "not pea protein… go eat steak" (verified, with an offensive aside, not reproduced).
 - For kidney disease: "crank up the protein" (verified).
 - On a high-dose fat-loss regimen, protein lets the liver make ApoB "trucks" to export fat `[paraphrase]`.
 - His per-pound targets (1.2–1.5 g/**lb**) far exceed the evidence `[literature]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`:
 - Leucine is the **"doorbell"** for mTORC1 (via the Sestrin2 sensor), which starts muscle protein synthesis.
 - The other EAAs are the **bricks**. Ringing the doorbell without bricks does little.
 - ~25–40 g of high-quality protein per meal (~2.5–3 g leucine) maximizes the response in most adults; older adults need more per meal ("anabolic resistance").
 - Muscle is the body's largest glucose disposal site, so preserving muscle preserves insulin sensitivity.
 - ApoB synthesis does need amino acids (a fair point), but there is no evidence that ultra-high protein prevents fatty liver.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A: protein + resistance training increases lean mass; benefit plateaus ~1.6 g/kg/day (95% CI up to ~2.2).
 - Grade A/B: higher protein during weight loss preserves lean mass.
 - Plant protein in adequate amounts works (slightly lower leucine; compensate with quantity), so "animal only" is preference, not physiology `[literature]`.
- **Dose:**
 - Healthy adults: 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (training up to ~2.0–2.2 g/kg in an energy deficit).
 - Older adults: 1.0–1.2 g/kg (1.2–1.5 with acute or chronic illness, per PROT-AGE).
 - 0.3–0.4 g/kg per meal over 3–4 meals.
 - EAA/leucine supplements only if whole-food protein is not feasible.
- **Hard exception:** **CKD (non-dialysis, eGFR <60): KDIGO advises ~0.8 g/kg/day and avoiding >1.3 g/kg; KDOQI 2020 allows 0.55–0.6 g/kg in metabolically stable CKD 3–5.** Protein targets in CKD are a **nephrologist/dietitian decision.** His "crank up the protein" for CKD stages 1–4 is contradicted by guidelines `[literature]`.
- **Risks:** high protein is harmful in CKD; GI upset from EAA powders. Gout patients: purine-heavy meat load.
- **HE:** לויצין = "פעמון הדלת" של בניית שריר, וחומצות האמינו החיוניות = הלבנים. 1.2–1.6 גרם לק"ג ביום, 25–40 גרם לארוחה. במחלת כליות כרונית רק לפי נפרולוג.

### S19. Collagen peptides
- **Class:** hydrolyzed protein fraction (rich in glycine, proline, hydroxyproline).
- **Big-Three:** INF (connective tissue repair). His routing: muscle preservation on GLP-1s.
- **His framing:** hydrolyzed collagen peptides "trip the signal"; "not intact collagen so not powder not bone broth" (verified fragment).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: hydrolysis yields absorbable di- and tri-peptides (Pro-Hyp, Hyp-Gly) that appear in blood. They act as **raw material plus a weak "rebuild" signal to fibroblasts and tenocytes**. Collagen is an **incomplete protein** (low leucine, no tryptophan), so it does not count toward the leucine/EAA target in S18.
- **Evidence:** grade C.
 - 15 g gelatin + vitamin C 1 h before loading raised a collagen-synthesis marker (small RCT).
 - 10 g/day reduced activity-related joint pain in athletes (small RCT).
 - Skin elasticity trials: modest, often industry-funded `[literature]`.
- **Dose:** 10–15 g/day. For tendon loading, take it ~30–60 min before the session with vitamin C-containing food `[literature]`.
- **Risks:** few. Allergy to the source animal or fish; kosher/halal source considerations `[inferred]`.
- **HE:** קולגן = חומר גלם לגידים ולעור, לא חלבון שלם. 10–15 גרם ביום. ראיות C.

### D1. Metformin
- **Class:** biguanide (Rx).
- **Big-Three:** IR.
- **His framing:**
 - "the wrecking ball drug" (verified).
 - It "puts your mitochondria into limp mode" (verified).
 - "they tell you that you have a metformin deficiency that doesn't even make sense" (verified).

At clinical levels it alters the cytosolic NADH/NAD+ redox state, acts on a lysosomal target and the gut (GLP-1 release, microbiome).  - Grade A for type 2 diabetes (first-line, >60 years of use, low cost).

 - Grade B: in older adults it blunts resistance-training hypertrophy and lean-mass gains (MASTERS, Walton 2019) and aerobic-training gains in insulin sensitivity and mitochondrial capacity (Konopka 2019). This is relevant for people told to train hard `[literature]`.

 - Lactic acidosis is rare (~3–10 per 100k person-years, near background); kidney-function-dependent prescribing.
 - Tell the radiology team and physician you take metformin before contrast scans or during acute illness; they decide whether to pause it.
- **Regulatory/Israel:** registered and in the health basket (several brands) `[inferred]`. Not WADA-prohibited.
- **Bottom line:** **a decision for a physician.** If glucose improves on lifestyle measures, *tell the prescriber*. Never self-taper.
- **HE:** מטפורמין מעמעם את ברז הסוכר בכבד (A לסוכרת, D לאריכות ימים). בשימוש ארוך לבדוק B12. לא מפסיקים לבד, ההחלטה של הרופא.

### D2. Statins (his critique vs the evidence)
- **Class:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (Rx).
- **Big-Three:** INF (lipids/atherosclerosis).
- **His framing:**
 - "there's not a day in in anyone's life where a statin has solved a problem" (verified).
 - "statins lower cholesterol which you don't need to do" (verified).
 - Claims Framingham "proved cholesterol is actually required and that 275 to 300 is healthy 160 to 180 is killing people" (verified as his words).
 - Says CRP is the better predictor; ApoB over LDL; "resolve the inflammation" (verified) instead.
 - Claims statins cause muscle wasting, cognitive decline, CoQ10 depletion, and kidney harm (proteinuria +23–25%, unverified citations).
 - Uses insulting language about prescribing doctors (not reproduced).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: block the liver's cholesterol factory, so the liver pulls in more LDL particles via **upregulated LDL receptors** (like opening more "loading docks"). They also lower CRP, and they block mevalonate products (CoQ10, prenyl groups), which is where his CoQ10 point comes from.
- **Where he is partly right** `[literature]`: ApoB is a better atherogenic-particle measure than LDL-C. Inflammation (CRP) adds independent risk. Plasma CoQ10 falls on statins. Some people get genuine muscle symptoms.
- **Where the evidence contradicts him** `[literature]`:
 - Large meta-analyses: each 1 mmol/L LDL-C reduction cuts major vascular events by ~22% (CTT).
 - The landmark "CRP trial" (JUPITER) was itself a **statin trial**: rosuvastatin in people with high CRP and normal LDL cut events ~44%.
 - Blinded data show most reported muscle symptoms are not caused by the statin (CTT 2022: statins account for a small fraction of muscle complaints in year 1; SAMSON n-of-1: ~90% nocebo).
 - Small real increase in new-onset diabetes risk.
 - Cognitive decline not supported by RCTs.
 - In CKD, SHARP was neutral for kidney progression and reduced atherosclerotic events.
 - Framingham did *not* show TC 275–300 as healthy; higher cholesterol tracked with more CHD.
- **Evidence grade:** A for secondary prevention and high-risk primary prevention.
- **Risks:** myopathy (rare true rhabdomyolysis), diabetes signal, liver enzyme rise, interactions (CYP3A4: berberine, grapefruit, some antibiotics/antifungals).
- **Bottom line:** **a decision for a physician.** Muscle pain on a statin → see the prescriber (options include switching, rechallenge, non-statin agents). Do not stop on your own, especially after a heart attack, stroke or stent.
- **HE:** סטטין פותח "רציפי טעינה" ל-LDL בכבד. ראיות A למניעת אירועים. ApoB ו-CRP חשובים (פה הוא צודק חלקית), אבל ניסוי ה-CRP עצמו היה ניסוי סטטין. כאבי שרירים = לרופא, לא להפסיק לבד.

### D3. SSRIs (balanced)
- **Class:** selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (Rx).
- **Big-Three:** INF (his routing: "inflamed brain").
- **His framing:**
 - "ssris force more serotonin into an already inflamed system" (verified).
 - "medicine uses ssris that work in less than 30% of patients and takes a minimum of eight to 10 weeks" (verified).
 - Claims frequent sexual dysfunction, anhedonia, and severe withdrawal.
 - Frames anxiety as "broken biology… about 95% of the time" `[paraphrase]`.
 - Uses derogatory language about psychiatrists; **excluded from all agent output.**
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: block the serotonin transporter (SERT), so serotonin stays longer in the synapse. Downstream, over weeks: 5-HT1A autoreceptor desensitization, BDNF and neuroplasticity changes, and HPA-axis normalization. The delay is about **rewiring, not filling a tank**. Inflammation-depression links are real and an active research area (a subgroup with high CRP responds worse to some SSRIs), but this does not mean "SSRIs can't work".
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A. A 2018 network meta-analysis of 522 trials found all 21 antidepressants more effective than placebo for major depression. Effects are moderate; response ~50% vs ~35–40% on placebo.
 - SSRIs are first-line for GAD, panic, OCD and PTSD.
 - "<30%" conflates *first-step remission* (STAR*D ~30%) with response.
 - Onset: partial effects often in 2–4 weeks; full effect 6–8+ weeks.
 - Side effects are real and should be named honestly: sexual dysfunction (common), emotional blunting, GI, sleep changes.
 - **Discontinuation symptoms are real** (2024 meta-analysis: ~31% vs ~17% on placebo; severe ~3%), so stopping must be tapered by the prescriber.
 - Omega-3, exercise, sleep and CBT have evidence as **adjuncts**; exercise and CBT are first-line options for mild-moderate cases, and omega-3 adds a small benefit. None replaces an SSRI without the treating clinician `[literature]`.
- **Risks:** serotonin syndrome with **methylene blue, 5-HTP, tramadol, triptans, linezolid, MAOIs**; bleeding with NSAIDs or anticoagulants; hyponatremia in older adults; activation or suicidality signal in under-25s (needs monitoring).
- **Bottom line:** **a decision for a physician or psychiatrist.** Never stop abruptly. Lifestyle and nutrient work is *alongside*, not instead.
- **HE:** SSRI משנים את המוח לאורך שבועות (נוירופלסטיות), לא "ממלאים מיכל". ראיות A, אפקט בינוני. תופעות לוואי ותסמיני הפסקה אמיתיים. אסור לשלב עם מתילן בלו או 5-HTP, ומפסיקים רק בהדרגה דרך הרופא.

### D4. Benzodiazepines, Z-drugs (Ambien) and sedating antihistamines
- **Class:** GABA-A positive allosteric modulators; diphenhydramine = anticholinergic H1 antagonist.
- **Big-Three:** INF (his routing: sleep/stress).

### D5. Testosterone (TRT)
- **Class:** androgen (Rx, controlled in many jurisdictions).
- **Big-Three:** IR, ATP (his routing).
- **His framing:**
 - "there are thousands of studies proving the efficacy and safety" (verified).

## B5. Lifestyle levers (exact numbers allowed, nothing extreme)

### L1. Eating window (time-restricted eating, "18/6")
- **Big-Three:** IR, ATP.
- **His framing:**
 - "fasting 18 6 not 16 8 18 6" (verified, i.e. "Not 16/8, 18/6").

 - Cites "Liu 2019 NEJM: −34% HOMA-IR" (see B8: the real Liu 2022 NEJM trial found **no added benefit** beyond calorie restriction).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: a long overnight fast lowers insulin, so fat is mobilized and liver glycogen is drawn down. Aligning eating with daytime circadian insulin sensitivity is plausible ("eat when the metabolic shop is open"). Autophagy claims in humans at 18 h are unproven.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade B. TRE is roughly equal to calorie restriction for weight and glycemia: TREAT (16:8) was null vs control; Liu 2022 NEJM found no difference vs CR alone.
 - **Early** TRE (eating ending mid-afternoon) improved insulin sensitivity and BP in small trials (Sutton 2018, Jamshed 2022).
 - 18:6 is not proven superior to 16:8.
- **Numbers (safe):** start with a 12 h overnight fast (e.g., 19:00–07:00). Progress to 14:10, then 16:8 if desired. 18:6 is optional for healthy adults who meet protein targets. An earlier window (e.g., 08:00–16:00 or 10:00–18:00) beats late eating.
- **Who must not self-start** `[literature]`:
 - **Anyone on insulin, sulfonylureas or SGLT2 inhibitors** (hypoglycemia / ketoacidosis risk) → physician first.
 - Type 1 diabetes.
 - Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
 - History of eating disorder.
 - Under 18; underweight or frail older adults.
 - Advanced CKD.
 - **Rule:** dizziness, glucose <70 mg/dL or confusion → eat, check, and contact the physician.
- **Prolonged water fasts** (he once advises 48 h): **not recommended without medical supervision**; never for people on glucose-lowering or BP drugs.
- **HE:** חלון אכילה עובד בעיקר כי אוכלים פחות. מוקדם עדיף על מאוחר. להתחיל ב-12 שעות צום לילה. 18:6 רשות, לא חובה. מי שמטופל באינסולין או סולפונילאוריאה, או בהריון: רק עם רופא.

### L4. Post-meal walking

### L7. Morning sunlight + caffeine timing
- **Big-Three:** INF (cortisol rhythm), ATP (circadian).
- **His framing:**

- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: morning light reaches melanopsin retinal cells, then the SCN "master clock", which sets the circadian phase (earlier sleep onset, earlier melatonin) and shapes the cortisol awakening response.

 - Caffeine within ~6 h of bedtime measurably disrupts sleep (grade B).
- **Numbers:** outdoors 10–30 min within the first hour after waking, eyes open but **not looking at the sun** (longer on overcast days). Caffeine ≤400 mg/day total (≈3–4 cups brewed coffee), last intake ≥8 h before bed. Delaying the first cup is optional.
- **Who must not:** photosensitizing drugs (some antibiotics, isotretinoin, amiodarone) and skin-cancer history → use skin protection. Bipolar disorder: light therapy can trigger mania → psychiatrist.
- **HE:** אור בוקר בחוץ 10–30 דקות לכיוון הסביבה, **אף פעם לא להסתכל ישר לשמש** (נזק לרשתית). קפאין עד 400 מ"ג ולא 8 שעות לפני שינה.

### L8. Sleep
- **Big-Three:** all three.
- **His framing:**
 - "just solve your damn sleep" (verified).
 - "make your room jet black turn off your tv make it cool yes it should be about 65 67" (°F, verified).
 - Constant bedtime.
 - "Sleep what your biology needs" (4–10 h; he says he sleeps 4–5 h) `[paraphrase]`.
 - Sleeping pills knock you out without real sleep (see D4).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: slow-wave sleep is the "night shift". The glymphatic system clears metabolites, GH pulses, and insulin sensitivity is restored. One night of short sleep measurably worsens next-day insulin sensitivity.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade A for harm from habitual <7 h (consensus statement; cohort data).
 - Grade A: CBT-I for insomnia.
 - **His "4–5 h is fine if that's your biology" is contradicted** except for a rare genetic short-sleeper minority.
 - "+30% amyloid after one night" overstates the PET data (~5%).
- **Numbers:** **7–9 h** in bed asleep for adults. Fixed wake time ±30 min, 7 days a week. Bedroom 18–19 °C (65–67 °F), dark, quiet. Screens off 30–60 min before bed. No alcohol within 3–4 h of bed.

- **HE:** 7–9 שעות. שעת קימה קבועה. חדר חשוך וקריר 18–19°C. נחירות או הפסקות נשימה = בדיקת דום נשימה דרך הרופא.

### L9. Cold and heat
- **Big-Three:** INF, ATP.
- **His framing:** minimal in the corpus. Sauna and heat-shock proteins appear only as one stress-reduction option (ASR: "heat shot proteins with sauna", paraphrased). **No first-party cold-plunge protocol found**; cold-plunge content in the corpus belongs to other creators `[inferred from search]`.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: heat induces heat-shock proteins (protein "chaperones") and plasma-volume and cardiovascular adaptations. Cold triggers a norepinephrine surge and brown-fat activation.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade C. Frequent sauna (4–7×/week) is associated with lower CV mortality (Finnish cohort; observational).
 - **Cold-water immersion right after strength training blunts hypertrophy and strength gains** (RCT): a direct conflict with his strength-first approach.
- **Numbers (not extreme):** sauna 80–90 °C, 10–20 min, 2–4×/week, hydrated, no alcohol. Cold: 10–15 °C water, 1–3 min. Keep cold ≥4–6 h away from strength sessions if muscle gain is the goal.
- **Who must not:** unstable angina, recent MI, severe aortic stenosis, uncontrolled hypertension, pregnancy (sauna), Raynaud's or cold urticaria (cold); never alone or after alcohol.
- **HE:** סאונה (ראיות C מתצפית) 10–20 דקות. מים קרים 1–3 דקות, לא מיד אחרי אימון כוח (מעכב את הגדילה). מחלת לב = קודם רופא.

### L10. Fructose (added/concentrated) reduction
- **Big-Three:** IR, INF.
- **His framing:**
 - Concentrated fructose "literally goes right to the liver and beats the [expletive] out of you" (verified).
 - Fruit is exempt because fiber is limiting `[paraphrase]`, but during fatty-liver "reversal" he bans even fruit "for now" `[his framing]`.
 - He gives a detailed (and largely correct) fructokinase → de novo lipogenesis → uric acid chain, with unverified percentages.
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: fructose skips the main glycolysis checkpoint (PFK), so the liver gets **"no speed limit"** carbon flow. That means more de novo lipogenesis, more VLDL and liver fat. ATP depletion drives uric acid formation.
- **Evidence:**
 - Grade B: fructose-sweetened drinks at ~25% of energy raised liver lipogenesis and visceral fat vs glucose (10-week RCT). Restricting fructose reduced liver fat in children within days.
 - Grade A: cutting sugar-sweetened beverages reduces weight gain.
 - Whole fruit: inverse association with T2D (grade C) and no RCT harm at normal intake. **No basis for banning whole fruit.**
- **Numbers:** added sugars <10% of energy (WHO ideal <5%, ≈25 g/day). Zero sugar-sweetened beverages and juice. Whole fruit 2–3 portions/day is fine, including in fatty liver.
- **HE:** פרוקטוז מרוכז = "בלי מעצור" לייצור שומן בכבד. לאפס משקאות ממותקים ומיצים. סוכר מוסף פחות מ-25–50 גרם ביום. פרי שלם מותר גם בכבד שומני.

### L11. Alcohol
- **Big-Three:** INF, ATP, IR.
- **His framing:** alcohol "instantly depletes glutathione" (verified). From a video description: "stop romanticizing alcohol because it's not your friend it's a slow motion arsonist" (verified, description text).
- **Real mechanism** `[literature]`: ethanol → acetaldehyde (toxic, carcinogenic). The NADH surge (redox jam) promotes liver fat. Glutathione depletion; gut-barrier leak and endotoxemia; disrupted slow-wave sleep.
- **Evidence:** grade A that alcohol causes cancer (WHO: no safe level for cancer risk) and that cutting it improves liver enzymes, BP and sleep. Older "moderate drinking is protective" data are confounded.
- **Numbers:** 0 is best. If drinking, ≤1 standard drink/day, not daily, none within 3–4 h of bed. **Fatty liver (MASLD) or elevated GGT/ALT → abstain and see the physician.** Anyone with dependence: **do not stop abruptly without medical support** (withdrawal can be dangerous).
- **HE:** אלכוהול: אפס זה הכי טוב. בכבד שומני להימנע לגמרי. מי שיש לו תלות לא מפסיק בבת אחת, אלא בליווי רפואי.

### L12. Hydration and salt
- **His framing:**
 - "stop drinking regular water… it screws with your blood plasma" (verified).
 - "i put salt in everything… salt in my coffee" (verified).
 - "it's not a sodium problem it's an insulin problem" (verified).
 - On GLP-1-class drugs, schedule water because thirst is suppressed `[paraphrase]` (reasonable).
- **Evidence and correction** `[literature]`: plain water does not "damage plasma". Sodium raises BP (grade A). Insulin-driven sodium retention is a *second* real mechanism, not a replacement. Scheduled fluids on appetite-suppressing drugs are sensible (dehydration → kidney injury risk).
- **Numbers:**
 - Total water intake ~2.7 L/day (women) and ~3.7 L/day (men) *from all sources including food* (NASEM AI); more in heat and exercise. Pale-yellow urine is the practical gauge.
 - Sodium per S17 (≤2,300 mg/day baseline; the small symptomatic low-carb exception only in the first 1–2 weeks, with normal BP and healthy heart and kidneys, and never with diuretics or RAAS drugs).
 - Sip through the day ("sip don't chug" is fine advice).
- **Who must not follow generic fluid/salt advice:** heart failure, CKD, cirrhosis, SIADH, diuretic users → physician-set fluid and sodium targets.
- **HE:** מים רגילים זה בסדר. 2.7–3.7 ליטר ביום מכל המקורות. נתרן עד 2,300 מ"ג. אי ספיקת לב או כליות: יעד נוזלים ומלח רק מהרופא.

### B6.2 If/then rules (all end in a safe action)
- New palpitations, chest pain, fainting, or a racing resting heart rate on any stack → **stop the new item(s) and see a physician today.**
- Glucose <70 mg/dL or symptoms of a low while on diabetes drugs → **treat the low, then contact the prescriber** (never self-adjust the drug).
- Dark urine, jaundice, right-upper-quadrant pain, or pale stools on curcumin, ashwagandha, berberine, black cohosh or TUDCA → **stop all supplements and get LFTs + physician review.**
- Fever, agitation, tremor, sweating or diarrhea after adding methylene blue, 5-HTP or rhodiola to a serotonergic drug → **emergency care (possible serotonin syndrome).**
- Creatinine/eGFR worse after starting creatine or high protein → **stop, retest with cystatin C, and see the physician.**
- ApoB/LDL markedly up on carnivore/low-carb → **stop the extreme version, retest in 6–8 weeks, physician review.**
- BP rising or ankle swelling after adding sodium or electrolyte drinks → **stop the added sodium and see the physician.**
- Hematocrit, PSA or BP rising on hormone therapy → **physician** (no self-titration).
- Persistent low mood, anxiety or insomnia despite the foundations → **physician or psychologist** (CBT/CBT-I, medication evaluation). Supplements are adjuncts.

### B6.3 Baseline labs worth requesting (Israel-aware)
- **Before or with supplements:** CBC, CMP (creatinine/eGFR, K, Na, ALT/AST/GGT), fasting glucose + fasting insulin (HOMA-IR = glucose mg/dL × insulin µU/mL / 405), HbA1c, lipid panel + ApoB if available, TSH (± FT4), B12 (+ MMA if borderline), ferritin, serum Mg.
- **25(OH)D only with an indication** (kupot do not test routinely) `[literature]`.
- **Before any iron:** ferritin + CBC (never empiric iron).
- **On metformin:** periodic B12. **On creatine:** tell the lab; cystatin C if eGFR is in question. **On hormones:** prescriber-directed panel (hematocrit, PSA, E2, lipids).
- Kupot generally require a physician referral for fasting insulin and ApoB. RBC Mg and the omega-3 index are usually private tests `[inferred, not verified]`.
