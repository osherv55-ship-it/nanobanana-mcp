## How to read this library

**Provenance tags.** These appear on every claim.
- **[his framing]** is how Dr Trevor Bachmeyer explains it. Sources are his videos, posts and website, taken from the research corpus.
- **[literature]** is what the independent evidence shows. Sources are trials, meta-analyses, drug labels and guidelines.
- **[inferred]** means the corpus has no protocol of his for this point. It is built from his framework, or is our own reasoning.

**Quotes.** A phrase appears in quotation marks only when it was found word for word in the raw transcript corpus. Anything else attributed to him is marked **[paraphrase]**. The hook and close lines are written *in his register*. They are not presented as his words.

**Evidence grades.**
- **A**: human RCTs or a meta-analysis for this indication.
- **B**: small RCTs, or surrogate endpoints.
- **C**: observational data, or small inconsistent trials.
- **D**: animal or in-vitro data only.

**Hard boundary.** This document gives no dose, amount, route, frequency, cycle length, timing, reconstitution or sourcing for:
- peptides and research compounds;
- prescription drugs and hormones.

That holds even when attributed to him ("he uses X") and even as a range. For those compounds you get six things: what it is, the real mechanism, what he claims, the evidence grade, the regulatory status and the key risks. Every one of them is **a decision for a physician**.

Supplements carry doses only when they fall within the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) and within the ranges used in human trials. Where he goes above a UL, we say so and stay below it. Where a supplement has no UL (curcumin, for example), we use the human-trial range and say so. Every if/then rule ends in a safe action: stop, retest, or see the physician.

**Units.**
- Vitamin D: ng/mL × 2.5 = nmol/L.
- hs-CRP is in mg/L. His "0.5" target is stated without a unit; it reads as mg/L.
- Insulin: µIU/mL = mU/L.
- Israeli labs often report vitamin D in nmol/L.

**Israel.** We could not verify Ministry of Health registration for any compound listed: the MoH drug registry was unavailable during research. As far as we know, no research peptide in this library is a registered drug in Israel [not verified]. Most standard markers named here can be ordered through the kupot. These include CBC, ferritin, CRP, HbA1c, lipids, TSH, 25(OH)D, tTG-IgA, calprotectin, testosterone, SHBG and AMH. Fasting insulin, AMH and some vitamin panels may need justification or a private lab [inferred, not verified].

# 1) ACUTE INJURY (muscle, tendon, ligament)

### Hook
**EN:** You didn't just tear a muscle. You opened a construction site with the power cut, and lying on the couch doesn't restart the generator.
**HE:** פציעה היא לא "תנוח וזה יעבור". זו פשיטת רגל אנרגטית בנקודה אחת בגוף, ומנוחה לא מחזירה את החשמל.

His own line: "an injury is a metabolic bankruptcy at a specific location in your body" [his framing].

### Big-Three routing

**Primary: power outage.** [his framing]
- Rebuilding costs ATP at every step: cell migration, protein synthesis, collagen cross-linking, VEGF secretion.
- The body runs "metabolic triage". Brain, heart, lungs and kidneys get energy first, then the immune response, and healing comes third. [paraphrase]
- With weak mitochondria, repair is rationed and collagen is laid down as scar.
- "energy is permission" [his framing].

**Secondary: fire.**
- [his framing] Acute inflammation should be "vigorous and appropriately scaled" and then shut off [paraphrase]. The failure is that it never resolves.
- [literature] The inflammatory phase is required. Neutrophils, then M1 macrophages, then the switch to M2 macrophages with pro-resolving mediators. Heavily blunting it may slow repair (C).

**Tertiary: fuel.**
- [literature] High glucose and AGEs impair fibroblast function and new blood vessel growth. Poor healing in diabetes is the textbook example (A).

**The spiral.**
- [his framing] Low ATP means inflammation can't finish. Unfinished inflammation burns more energy. Less energy means more scar.
- [literature] Hyperglycaemia leads to glycated collagen and impaired angiogenesis, which prolong inflammation, which worsens insulin signalling.

### Mechanism chain [literature]
1. **Haemostasis and platelet degranulation (PDGF, TGF-β)** → the 911 call. Platelets ring the alarm and dispatch the crews.
2. **Neutrophils, then M1 macrophages clear debris (days 0–5)** → the demolition crew.
3. **M1→M2 switch; resolvins and protectins end inflammation** → the foreman calls off demolition. If he never calls it off, you get a chronic injury (see §2).
4. **Fibroblasts lay down type III collagen; VEGF drives new vessels (weeks 1–6)** → scaffolding and temporary wiring.
5. **Mechanotransduction: load → integrin/FAK signalling → collagen III converts to aligned type I (weeks 6 to 12+ months)** → the building inspector only approves what has been stress-tested. This is why loading, not bed rest, finishes the job.
6. **Leucine → mTORC1 → protein synthesis, all ATP- and amino-acid-dependent** → no bricks, no building.

Note [literature]: muscle remodels in weeks. Tendon and ligament remodel over months, up to a year. Being pain-free is not the same as being remodelled.

### Tier 0: foundations
- **Load, don't park.** Protect for 1–3 days, then progressive loading guided by pain. Pain during exercise ≤3/10, back to baseline by the next morning (B) [literature]. "rest does not repair tissue" [his framing]. The literature agrees after the protection phase. It does not agree with skipping diagnosis.
- **Train what isn't injured:** 3 sessions a week.
- **Steps:** 6,000–8,000 a day if the injury allows (cycling or swimming if not).
- **Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day, spread over 4 meals of about 0.4 g/kg (25–40 g each, including ~2.5–3 g leucine). **Eat at maintenance calories.** A deficit slows repair (B). Kidney disease (eGFR <60 or albuminuria) → protein target set by your physician/dietitian.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h with a fixed wake time.
- **Morning light:** 10–20 min outdoors within 1 h of waking.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h. No extended fasting in the first 2–3 weeks of repair [inferred].
- **Cut:** alcohol, which blunts muscle protein synthesis after exercise (B). Also nicotine and smoking, which impair tendon and bone healing (A, observational).
- **Hydration:** about 30–35 mL/kg/day, more in heat. Heart or kidney disease → fluid limit set by your physician.
- **Track weekly:** pain 0–10, range of motion versus the other side, function, sleep. "a short pencil is way better than a long memory" [his framing].

### Tier 2: prescription and clinical options to raise with a physician
- **Diagnosis first.** Some injuries are time-sensitive: complete Achilles rupture, a full-thickness cuff tear in an active person, an unstable knee, possible fracture. Imaging and a surgical opinion have windows (A, injury-specific).
 - [his framing] He says to start immediately and not wait for the MRI.
 - [literature] Our position: get the diagnosis. Some repairs lose options with delay.
- **Pain control that enables loading.**
 - Acetaminophen, or a *short* NSAID course: A for pain.
 - Prolonged NSAIDs may slow bone and tendon healing: C.
 - Duration is a physician decision. He dismisses RICE, NSAIDs and cortisone [his framing].
- **Physiotherapy with progressive loading:** A for many muscle and tendon injuries.
- **PRP:** C. Mixed results, injury-specific.
- **Corticosteroid injection:** short-term relief in some tendinopathies (B). Not into tendon substance, and repeated injections weaken tissue. Physician procedure only.

### Labs
Labs are usually unnecessary for a simple strain [literature]. Order them for slow healing, repeated injuries or metabolic risk.

**Recheck:** only abnormal markers, at 8–12 weeks.

**If/then:**
- Ferritin <30 ng/mL → see the physician for an iron work-up and cause. Do not self-start high-dose iron.
- 25(OH)D <20 ng/mL → physician-guided repletion; retest at 8–12 weeks.
- HbA1c ≥5.7% → tighten Tier 0, physician review, retest at 3 months.
- hs-CRP >10 mg/L with no obvious cause → retest in 2–3 weeks. Still high → physician.

### Checkpoints (things to check, not promises)
- **Week 2:** swelling settling; pain down ≥2 points; sleep less disrupted; daily activities close to normal.
- **Week 4:** progressive loading tolerated without next-day flare; range of motion approaching the other side.
- **Week 8:** muscle strains — strength testing begins against the other side. A common return-to-sport criterion is ≥90% of the other side [literature]. Tendons and ligaments are still remodelling.
- **Week 12:** function and sport-specific drills. If you are stalled, re-image or re-assess.
- [his framing] His projection of being nearly fully recovered by week 6 is a *projection*, not trial data.

### Red flags: stop and see a doctor
- Can't bear weight or take 4 steps, visible deformity, or suspected fracture.
- An audible pop with immediate weakness: tendon rupture, e.g. Achilles or biceps.
- Numbness, a cold or pale limb, or pain out of proportion with tense swelling (compartment syndrome) → **ER**.
- Fever, spreading redness or red streaks, especially after any injection.
- Calf swelling or pain after immobilisation (DVT).
- Dark urine after crush or extreme exertion.
- No improvement at 2–3 weeks.

### Close
**EN:** Healing isn't a vacation, it's work. Feed the work, load it smart, write it down. The variable is you.
**HE:** ריפוי זה לא חופשה, זו עבודה. תזין אותה, תעמיס חכם, תרשום הכול. המשתנה זה אתה.

# 2) CHRONIC INJURY / POOR HEALING

### Hook
**EN:** That knee that's hurt for two years isn't "just how it is". It's a repair job that ran out of budget halfway and never got re-funded.
**HE:** הברך שכואבת לך כבר שנתיים לא "התרגלה לכאב". היא תקועה באמצע תיקון שאף אחד לא מימן עד הסוף.

### Big-Three routing

**Primary: fire.** [his framing]
- Chronic injury is chronic inflammation. TNF-α, IL-6 and CRP stay elevated at a low, inefficient level ("inflammaging").
- In his words, it's "like a catch 22 your body is inflamed because it's trying to heal but the inflammation prevents the healing because there isn't enough energy to move from the inflammatory phase to the repair phase".

**Co-primary: power.** [his framing]
- Physio fails not because of willpower but because of mitochondrial capacity [paraphrase].

**Fuel.** [literature] Insulin resistance and diabetes stiffen collagen through AGE cross-links and slow repair (B/C).

**What the literature adds.** [literature]
- Most chronic tendinopathy is a *failed-healing / degenerative* continuum: disorganised collagen, nerve and vessel in-growth. It is not classic inflammation.
- Persistent pain often involves **central sensitisation**.
- Physio "fails" most often because the loading was under-dosed or not progressed, because of adherence, or because the diagnosis was wrong.

**The spiral.** Load beyond capacity → micro-damage → low-grade inflammation and sensitised nerves → you stop loading → the tissue loses capacity → the next load is even more "beyond capacity".

### Mechanism chain [literature]
1. **Load exceeds tissue capacity repeatedly** → withdrawals bigger than deposits.
2. **Reactive → disrepair → degenerative tendinopathy (more proteoglycan, disorganised collagen, neovessels and nerves)** → scaffolding built by a crew with no foreman.
3. **Persistent IL-6 / TNF-α, plus senescent cells secreting SASP** → a smoke alarm that never resets.
4. **Central sensitisation** → the volume knob stuck on high: the same signal hurts more.
5. **AGE cross-links and adipokines from visceral fat** → rust in the cables.
6. **Deconditioning** → unloaded tissue is dismantled. The body doesn't fund what you don't use.

### Tier 0: foundations
- **Progressive heavy loading:** heavy-slow resistance or isometrics for tendinopathy, 3×/week for **at least 12 weeks** (A/B).
 - Pain-monitoring model: ≤5/10 during the exercise is acceptable if it settles within 24 h (B).
 - "you're making your pt more effective" [his framing]. Agreed: compounds never replace this.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (kidney disease, eGFR <60 or albuminuria → protein target set by your physician/dietitian); **steps** 7,000–9,000; **strength training** for the rest of the body 2–3×/week.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. **Morning light:** 10–20 min.
- **Weight:** if overweight, a 5–10% loss reduces load and inflammatory signalling (A for knee OA; C for tendinopathy).
- **Cut:** sugary drinks and juice; alcohol; smoking.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **Track** a function score monthly: VISA-A for Achilles, VISA-P for knee, or a simple single-leg capacity test.

### Tier 2: to raise with a physician
- **Re-check the diagnosis** with ultrasound or MRI. Rule out systemic causes: spondyloarthritis, gout, hypothyroidism, diabetes, and fluoroquinolone-associated tendinopathy.
- **Shockwave (ESWT)** for calcific shoulder, plantar fasciopathy or lateral elbow: B.
- **PRP:** C, mixed results.
- **Topical NSAIDs** for superficial tendons and joints: B. **Glyceryl trinitrate (a nitrate)** for some tendinopathies: C, prescription only; the formulation is the physician's choice.
- **Duloxetine** where central sensitisation dominates: B.
- **Sports-medicine or rheumatology referral** if the pattern is inflammatory.
- [his framing] He says not to suppress inflammation with NSAIDs or prednisone. The literature supports avoiding *chronic* suppression. Short courses are a physician's call.

### Tier 3: compounds he points to (no dosing)
- **Same set as §1:** BPC-157, injectable NAD+ and MOTS-c, which he names for chronic injury; plus TB-500 and GHK-Cu.
 - [his framing] He claims energy lets inflammation "resolve" (M1→M2) instead of being suppressed.
 - [literature] All D. Status as in the regulatory snapshot.
- **KPV.**
 - [his framing] Low-grade inflammation control "if you're really hard on your body" [paraphrase].
 - [literature] A tripeptide from the C-terminus of α-MSH. Anti-inflammatory in mouse colitis and cell models. No human musculoskeletal data. D. PCAC 8–6; WADA S0 catch-all.
- **A decision for a physician.**

**Recheck:** abnormal markers at 12 weeks.

**If/then:**
- hs-CRP >3 mg/L twice → physician (look for a systemic cause).
- Uric acid high with episodic hot joints → physician (gout).
- Tendon pain while on a fluoroquinolone → **stop loading and call the prescriber the same day**.

### Checkpoints
- **Week 2:** a loading baseline that doesn't flare for more than 24 h.
- **Week 4:** pain on the provocation test (hop, squat or press) trending down.
- **Week 8:** measurable capacity gain, e.g. more single-leg calf-raise reps or a heavier load.
- **Week 12:** function score improved. If not, re-examine the diagnosis rather than adding compounds.

### Red flags
- Night or rest pain with weight loss or fever.
- Several swollen joints, or morning stiffness over 45–60 min.
- Neurological deficit: weakness, numbness, bladder or bowel change.
- Failure of 3 months of well-dosed rehab.
- Tendon pain on fluoroquinolones or steroids.

### Close
**EN:** Two years of pain isn't your sentence. It's an unfinished job. Finish it with load, sleep and patience. Your 80-year-old self is counting on you.
**HE:** שנתיים של כאב זה לא גזר דין. זו עבודה שלא נגמרה. תסיים אותה עם עומס, שינה וסבלנות. האדם שתהיה בגיל 80 סומך עליך.

# 3) POST-SURGERY / POST-ILLNESS RECOVERY

### Hook
**EN:** The surgery ended in the OR. The recovery starts the minute your feet hit the floor, and muscle is the bank account you survive on.
**HE:** הניתוח נגמר בחדר הניתוח. ההחלמה מתחילה ברגע שהרגליים נוגעות ברצפה, והשריר הוא חשבון הבנק שממנו אתה שורד.

### Big-Three routing

**His template (his own lung surgery).** [his framing]
- He was out of bed within hours, walking laps, and discharged in about half the expected time.
- "you demand adaptation and it will adapt".
- Muscle is the survival buffer [paraphrase].
- For organ recovery he says energy is the rate-limiting step. Even he says that version needs supervised care [paraphrase].

**Power.** [literature]
- Surgery and serious infection drive hypermetabolism and protein breakdown.
- About 10 days of bed rest costs older adults roughly a kilogram of leg lean mass (Kortebein 2007). Mitochondrial content drops with disuse.

**Fire.** [literature] The systemic inflammatory response: an IL-6 surge, acute-phase proteins, iron locked away by hepcidin.

**Fuel.** [literature] Post-operative insulin resistance is a well-described stress state. ERAS pre-op carbohydrate loading exists to blunt it (B).

**The spiral.** Stress hormones → insulin resistance → muscle catabolism → bed rest → less mitochondrial capacity → fatigue → more bed rest.

### Mechanism chain [literature]
1. **Surgical or infectious insult → IL-6, cortisol and catecholamine surge** → the body declares martial law.
2. **Stress hormones → post-operative insulin resistance (days to weeks)** → the fuel locks jam.
3. **Muscle proteolysis supplies amino acids for immunity and acute-phase proteins** → raiding the national reserve.
4. **Immobility → anabolic resistance and mitochondrial loss** → unused power plants get decommissioned.
5. **Hepcidin ↑ → iron sequestration → functional anaemia** → fewer delivery trucks on the road.
6. **Re-loading plus protein restore muscle protein synthesis** → reopening the factory.

### Tier 0: foundations (always inside your surgical or medical team's limits)
- **Early mobilisation as allowed** (ERAS, A).
 - Start with 5–10 min walks several times a day.
 - Add 2–5 min per day.
 - Aim for ≥5,000 steps/day by weeks 2–4 if your team allows.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day (ESPEN range for surgical and acutely ill patients). Adequate total energy: this is not the time for a deficit. Kidney disease (eGFR <60 or albuminuria) → protein target set by your physician/dietitian.
- **Breathing:** incentive spirometry or breathing exercises if prescribed. His story included tracking his own oxygen saturation [his framing].
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Daylight exposure in the morning, darkness at night.
- **Cut:** alcohol for at least 4 weeks; smoking, absolutely (A for wound complications).
- **Hydration:** as your team directs (heart or kidney patients may have limits).

# 10) PCOS

### Hook
**EN:** PCOS isn't an ovary problem. The ovaries are just the ring. The fight is about insulin.
**HE:** PCOS היא לא מחלה של השחלות. השחלות הן רק הזירה, והקרב האמיתי הוא על אינסולין.

His words: "it's not an ovarian disease it's a metabolic disorder that uses the ovaries as a boxing ring" [his framing].

### Big-Three routing

**His framing.** [his framing]
- Insulin resistance comes first, as a by-product of systemic inflammation, which causes mitochondrial dysfunction.
- Hyperinsulinaemia overstimulates the ovaries → excess androgens → cysts, no ovulation, infertility.
- Metformin "coaxes" the liver; "retatrutide doesn't coax baby it obliterates" [his framing].
- Cysts, hair growth and infertility are symptoms, not the disease [paraphrase].

**What the literature supports.** [literature]
- Insulin resistance and hyperinsulinaemia are central in most women with PCOS, **including many lean women** (A/B).
- Chronic low-grade inflammation is elevated (C).
- Mitochondrial dysfunction has been described in muscle and oocytes (C).

**What the literature adds.** [literature]
- PCOS is **heterogeneous**.
- There is a primary neuroendocrine component: fast GnRH pulses and a high LH:FSH ratio.
- There is genetics: GWAS loci such as FSHB, LHCGR and DENND1A.
- There is AMH excess.
- So it is not *only* metabolic.

**The spiral.** Insulin resistance → hyperinsulinaemia → androgens ↑ and SHBG ↓ → visceral fat → inflammation → worse insulin resistance.

### Mechanism chain [literature]
1. **Insulin resistance in muscle and fat → compensatory hyperinsulinaemia** → the pancreas has to shout to be heard.
2. **Insulin + LH act on ovarian theca cells → CYP17 → androgen synthesis ↑** → turning up the testosterone factory.
3. **Hyperinsulinaemia lowers the liver's SHBG output → more free testosterone** → the guards holding the hormones in custody get laid off.
4. **Fast GnRH pulses → LH:FSH ↑; follicles arrest; AMH high → no ovulation → no progesterone** → follicles stuck at the starting line, and the uterine lining is never cleared.
5. **Androgens → hirsutism, acne, scalp thinning, more visceral fat** → collateral damage from the fight.
6. **Long-term risks:** type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, fatty liver, sleep apnoea, endometrial cancer, depression, pregnancy complications → the bill comes later.

### Tier 0: foundations (in line with the 2023 International Evidence-Based PCOS Guideline)
- **Weight:** if overweight, a 5–10% loss improves cycles and metabolic markers (B). **Lean PCOS is real.** Fix the fuel failure without chasing weight loss.
- **Exercise:** 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 min vigorous, plus 2 days of muscle strengthening.
- **Steps:** 8,000–10,000/day.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day. Kidney disease (eGFR <60 or albuminuria) → protein target set by your physician/dietitian.
- **Fibre:** 25–35 g/day. Carbohydrate quality over carbohydrate fear. The guideline finds no single diet superior.
- **Cut:** sugary drinks, juice, refined snacks; alcohol low.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Screen for sleep apnoea (snoring, daytime sleepiness).
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h. Early time-restricted eating has small trials (C).
- **Mental health:** depression, anxiety and disordered eating are common in PCOS. Screen, don't skip.

### Red flags
- Rapid virilisation: deepening voice, clitoral enlargement, fast-growing body hair.
- No period for over 3 months, or heavy prolonged bleeding.
- Pregnancy.
- Low mood, self-harm thoughts, or disordered eating.
- Loud snoring with daytime sleepiness.
- Symptoms of high blood sugar: thirst, frequent urination, weight loss.
