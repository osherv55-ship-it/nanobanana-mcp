## 0. Read-first: rules that apply to every protocol below

1. **Peptides, research compounds, prescription drugs and hormones get no doses here.** That covers amounts, routes, frequencies, cycles, timing, reconstitution and sourcing. It applies even when attributed to him. His doses vary several-fold between videos, and for most of these compounds no validated human dose exists [literature]. Each one is a decision for a physician.
2. **Supplement doses stay at or below the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL)** and within what human trials used. Where his own numbers go above a UL (zinc 50 mg, vitamin D 5,000–10,000 IU, magnesium 500 mg supplemental), this library flags it and does not recommend it.
3. **Never stop or change a prescribed medication on your own.** Every if/then rule below ends in one of three actions: stop the supplement, retest, or see the physician.
4. **One change at a time, then two to four weeks of observation.** In his words, "start small" and "you can't untake" something [his framing]. In practice, if you add three things at once you can't tell which one caused a side effect.
5. **Lab availability in Israel [inferred, verify with your health fund (kupa)].** Standard markers are routine through the health funds: lipids, HbA1c, TSH, free T4, eGFR, UACR, CBC, ferritin and 25(OH)D. Some may need a physician's justification or a private lab: ApoB, Lp(a), cystatin C, fasting insulin, free T3, SHBG and sensitive estradiol. Some are usually not routine at all: reverse T3, TNF-α, IL-6, FGF23, urine β2-microglobulin, urine RBP and PEth.
6. **The protein targets assume healthy kidneys.** The 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day targets in Problems 1, 2 and 6–10 are for normal kidney function. If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
7. **His vitamin D targets are not goals.** The lab tables show his 25(OH)D targets (40–80 or 60–80 ng/mL) as his numbers only. Above 50 ng/mL there is no proven benefit and possible harm (IOM). Don't dose above the UL to chase it.

## 1. Low testosterone, TRT, and coming off TRT

### Hook
- **EN:** Low T is usually a signal, fuel and cortisol problem before it's a testicle problem, and coming off TRT is a biology exam, not a mood.
- **HE:** טסטוסטרון נמוך זה לא תמיד האשכים. לפעמים זה המוח שהפסיק לשלוח הודעות כי הגוף עסוק בלשרוד.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fuel (insulin resistance) is the primary driver of functional low T.** He says it directly: "high insulin equals high aromatase" [his framing]. More visceral fat means more aromatase, which means more testosterone turned into estradiol. Estradiol then suppresses the hypothalamus. Obesity and type 2 diabetes are leading causes of functional secondary hypogonadism, and weight loss raises testosterone [literature].
- **Fire (stress and inflammation) adds to it.** "Cortisol is the antithesis to testosterone." The stress axis and the reproductive axis compete at the hypothalamus, and "you cannot biochemically restart your testosterone" while in a cortisol-dominant state [his framing]. Inflammatory cytokines and stress hormones (CRH, endogenous opioids) suppress GnRH pulses [literature].
- **Power outage (ATP shortage).** Leydig cells are steroid factories, and their first step happens inside mitochondria [literature]. He adds that when T crashes, the body reads it as a threat [his framing].
- **The spiral:** low T leads to muscle loss, which worsens insulin resistance, which raises aromatase, which lowers T further, while poor sleep drives cortisol up.
- **Coming off TRT [his framing]:** "you need labs, not bro science." He says that for many long-term users, coming off is "an ideological fantasy." His closing advice is to stay on TRT if you can't meet the prerequisites, because that's "wisdom," and "the goal isn't natural testosterone." He treats the decision as testes vs pituitary: either "your testicles are the failure point" or "the pituitary is fine."
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - His on-TRT target (900–1,200 ng/dL) sits at or above the top of the harmonized reference range. The Endocrine Society targets mid-normal levels on therapy, and supraphysiologic levels raise the risk of high hematocrit (erythrocytosis). It is not a number to dose toward; the prescriber sets the target [literature].
 - His cortisol "gate" for coming off TRT (fixed AM/PM thresholds) is his own construct. No trial has validated it as a predictor of a successful restart.
 - Aromatase inhibitors are off-label in men and are not a guideline treatment for hypogonadism. Over-suppressed estradiol harms bone, libido and lipids [literature]. Starting one is only ever the physician's call, never a self-rule tied to an estradiol number.
 - Kisspeptin is a research probe of the axis, not a proven restart therapy. Continuous exposure desensitizes the axis [literature].
 - Staying on TRT or coming off is an individual decision made with the prescriber. Recovery of the axis after stopping varies widely and can take many months [literature].

### Mechanism chain
1. **Kisspeptin neurons drive GnRH pulses.** Kisspeptin is the conductor, GnRH is the metronome. A steady signal deafens the receptor, so the axis needs pulses [literature].
2. **GnRH tells the pituitary to release LH and FSH.** LH and FSH are the work orders sent from HQ to the factory.
3. **LH binds Leydig cells, triggering cAMP signalling and the StAR protein, which moves cholesterol into mitochondria.** That's raw material loaded onto the factory conveyor. On TRT, the work orders stop and the factory goes idle; years without hCG can shrink it [literature, direction confirmed; magnitude debated].
4. **SHBG binds testosterone.** He calls it testosterone "thrown in prison" [his framing]. Only the free fraction works.
5. **Aromatase converts testosterone into estradiol, mostly in fat.** Fat is a second factory that counterfeits the product.
6. **Estradiol and testosterone feed back to the hypothalamus and pituitary to shut down GnRH, LH and FSH.** It's the thermostat. Outside testosterone tells it the house is warm enough, so the furnace switches off.

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Restricting sleep to about 5 h for one week lowered daytime testosterone 10–15% in young men [literature]. Get screened for sleep apnea (STOP-BANG). Untreated severe apnea is a contraindication to starting TRT [literature].
- **Body fat:** losing 5–10% of body weight is the single biggest lever for functional low T [literature, A for raising T].
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, split into 3–4 meals of ~0.4 g/kg each. If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Walking:** 7,000–10,000 steps/day, plus a 10–15 min walk after the largest meal (better glucose disposal).
- **Strength training:** 3×/week, compound lifts, progressive overload.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h, last meal 2–3 h before bed. If you take glucose-lowering drugs, don't fast without a physician.
- **Morning light:** 10–20 min outdoors within an hour of waking (anchors the cortisol rhythm).
- **Cut:** alcohol (aim for ≤2 drinks/week, ideally none), sugary drinks, ultra-processed snacks. Review opioids and high-dose glucocorticoids with your physician; both suppress the axis. Never stop them yourself.

### Tier 2 prescription options to raise with a physician
- **Testosterone therapy.** Restores androgen-receptor signalling. **A**, for symptomatic hypogonadism confirmed by two low morning fasting tests plus a workup for the cause. TRAVERSE showed no rise in major cardiac events, but more atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, acute kidney injury and fractures [literature].
- **Enclomiphene / clomiphene (SERMs).** Block estradiol feedback at the hypothalamus so LH and FSH rise and sperm production is preserved. **B**. Off-label in men.
- **hCG.** Mimics LH. Keeps Leydig cells active and preserves fertility on TRT or during a restart. **B** for fertility and preserving the axis. Off-label in men.
- **Aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole).** Blocks aromatase. **C** in men. Risks over-suppressing estradiol, which hurts bone, libido and lipids. He calls it "the nuclear option, last resort," and the literature agrees [his framing + literature].
- **Dopamine agonist, if prolactin is high.** Lowers prolactin, which unblocks GnRH. **A** for prolactinoma. Pituitary MRI if indicated.
- **GLP-1 agonist when obesity drives the low T.** Weight loss lowers aromatase and raises T. **B** for the T endpoint.
- **A physician-led restart plan when coming off TRT** (taper, bridge, monitoring). **B/C**: observational series only.

**Recheck cadence:** On TRT, check T, hematocrit and PSA at 3–6 months, then every year (Endocrine Society). During a restart, the prescriber typically checks LH/FSH, T and estradiol every 4–8 weeks.

**If/then rules (all end in a safe action):**
- Hematocrit ≥54% → contact the prescriber within days. Don't adjust anything yourself.
- PSA rises by more than 1.4 ng/mL within 12 months, or a nodule is felt → urology referral.
- Prolactin is high, or you have headaches or vision changes → physician this week. Pituitary imaging is their call.
- During a restart, LH/FSH stay flat, or mood drops sharply → retest, then physician. Don't add compounds.
- Breast lump or tenderness → retest estradiol and see the physician. Never start an aromatase inhibitor yourself.

### Checkpoints
- **Week 2:** Sleep and alcohol logs are steady. Baseline labs are drawn, twice for T.
- **Week 4:** Waist measurement, energy and libido scores. Review results with the physician; decide on diagnosis and cause.
- **Week 8:** First follow-up labs if treatment started or a restart is under way. Look at hematocrit and estradiol.
- **Week 12:** Body composition, strength numbers and a symptom questionnaire. Decide together whether to continue, adjust or stop. A restart can take 6–12 months; this is not a finish line.

### Red flags
Hematocrit ≥54%. Chest pain, leg swelling or sudden breathlessness (clot). Low mood with suicidal thoughts during a restart (urgent). Testicular mass. New visual-field loss or severe headache. Gynecomastia with a discrete lump. Severe snoring with daytime sleepiness while on TRT.

## 2. Age-related GH decline and sarcopenia

### Hook
- **EN:** You're not losing muscle because you're 50. You're losing it because the signal went quiet and you stopped giving the tissue a reason to stay.
- **HE:** את השריר לא מאבדים בגלל הגיל. מאבדים אותו כי הפסקתם לתת לו סיבה להישאר.

### Big-Three routing
- **Power outage is primary [his framing].** He calls GH "a master key to every single repair system" and synthetic GH "a wrecking ball" that shuts down your own production.
- **Fuel:** high insulin suppresses GH secretion. Visceral fat lowers GH pulses. Muscle is the main place glucose gets disposed of, so losing it worsens insulin resistance [literature].
- **Fire:** inflammaging (IL-6, TNF-α) promotes muscle protein breakdown and anabolic resistance [literature].
- **The spiral:** less muscle → worse glucose control → more visceral fat → lower GH → less muscle repair.
- **On labs:** IGF-1 "within normal limits is a lie" [his framing].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - Higher IGF-1 is not a proven health benefit. Lifelong low IGF-1 signalling is linked to longevity in model organisms, and IGF-1 drives some cancers. Strength and lean mass are the real targets, not IGF-1 [literature].
 - None of the GH secretagogues he names has shown a functional benefit (strength, falls, independence) in trials. MK-677 raised fasting glucose, and a hip-fracture trial was stopped early over a heart-failure signal [literature].
 - Resistance training with adequate protein is the best-supported intervention for age-related muscle loss [literature, A].

### Mechanism chain
1. **GHRH from the hypothalamus releases GH from the pituitary; somatostatin brakes it.** Accelerator and brake.
2. **GH pulses peak in slow-wave sleep.** The night shift does most of the work [literature].
3. **GH makes the liver produce IGF-1, which activates PI3K–Akt–mTORC1 in muscle.** IGF-1 is the contractor, mTOR is the building permit.
4. **Leucine plus mechanical load also activate mTORC1.** Lifting and protein are separate permit offices; you need both stamps.
5. **With age comes anabolic resistance: the same protein dose triggers less muscle protein synthesis.** The permit office has gone slow, so you need a bigger stack of paperwork per meal (~0.4 g/kg).
6. **The ubiquitin–proteasome system, driven by inflammation, disuse and energy deficit, tears down muscle.** The demolition crew works overtime when the site is idle [literature; he applies this to GLP-1 use].

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Resistance training:** 2–3×/week, all major muscle groups, 2–4 sets of 6–12 reps near failure, progressive overload. **Grade A** for strength and lean mass in older adults.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (up to 1.6 if training hard or in a calorie deficit). 3–4 meals of ~0.4 g/kg, each with ~2.5–3 g leucine (about 30–40 g of whey, or 120–150 g of meat or fish). If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day. Add balance work 2×/week if over 65.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Consistent bedtime. Dark, cool room (~18–19 °C).
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h. Don't eat a large meal in the 2–3 h before bed (insulin blunts the early-sleep GH pulse; physiology [literature]).
- **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight.
- **Cut:** alcohol (it suppresses GH during sleep), sugary drinks, long sitting (break it every 30–45 min).
- **On a GLP-1 drug:** strength training plus the protein targets above are non-negotiable. Ask for a DEXA scan before and during treatment.

## 3. Cardiovascular: cholesterol, ApoB, CRP, arterial inflammation

### Hook
- **EN:** Cholesterol isn't the whole story. Inflammation breaks the door open and ApoB particles walk through it. Measure both or you're blind.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fire is primary [his framing].** "Cholesterol isn't the cause." He says inflammation is the driver, and that CRP predicts risk better than LDL. He describes cytokines turning on endothelial "landing pads," so arteries get "chemically dismantled" from the inside.
- **Fuel:** insulin resistance raises triglycerides, lowers HDL and creates small dense LDL. He says that if you start retatrutide or MOTS-c "without knowing your fasting insulin or APOB, you're essentially blind firing" [his framing].
- **Power:** the endothelium needs ATP and nitric oxide to stay sealed; heart muscle is mitochondria-dense. "It's an ATP problem" (on heart failure) [his framing].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - ApoB-containing lipoproteins (LDL, VLDL remnants, Lp(a)) are **causal** for atherosclerosis. Genetic, epidemiological and RCT evidence agree, and lowering LDL reduces events in proportion [literature, A].

### Mechanism chain
1. **Apolipoprotein B (ApoB), one per atherogenic particle.** He calls it "a protein suitcase" [his framing]. Count the suitcases, not just the cholesterol inside them.
2. **The particles cross the endothelium and get stuck to proteoglycans in the vessel wall.** Trucks crash through a damaged fence and get stuck in the mud.
3. **Trapped lipoproteins oxidize; NF-κB in the endothelium switches on ICAM-1, VCAM-1 and E-selectin.** Landing pads light up for immune cells [his framing + literature].
4. **Monocytes enter, become macrophages, eat oxidized LDL and become foam cells.** The cleanup crew gorges and dies on the job, leaving a garbage pile (the necrotic core).
5. **Macrophage MMPs digest the fibrous cap; IL-1β/IL-6 amplify the reaction (measured as CRP).** Termites in the support beam.
6. **The cap ruptures, a clot forms, and you get a heart attack or stroke.** Most events come from rupture, not from a pipe slowly closing [literature].

- **Cut:** sugary drinks and added fructose (drives triglycerides and VLDL), trans fats, alcohol (raises TG and BP), smoking (non-negotiable; **A**).
- **Aerobic exercise:** 150–300 min/week moderate, or 75–150 min vigorous. Plus 2×/week resistance training.
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Screen for sleep apnea if you snore.
- **Weight:** losing 5–10% if overweight lowers TG, raises HDL and lowers CRP.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **Morning light:** 10–20 min.

## 4. Hypertension

### Hook
- **EN:** High blood pressure isn't the disease, it's the alarm. Lower the number, absolutely, and then go find out why it's ringing.
- **HE:** לחץ דם גבוה הוא לא המחלה. הוא האזעקה. מורידים את המספר, ואז מחפשים למה היא צלצלה.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fuel is primary [his framing].** "Hypertension isn't a disease"; it's "a compensatory symptom of broken internal terrain." His chain: insulin resistance → sympathetic overdrive → more norepinephrine → vasoconstriction, and the kidneys hold on to sodium.
- **Fire:** endothelial inflammation → less nitric oxide → stiffer arteries [his framing + literature].
- **Power:** low endothelial ATP and NO bioavailability, and the RAAS "alarm" [his framing].
- **The spiral:** stiff arteries → higher pressure → more endothelial injury → more inflammation → worse insulin sensitivity.
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - The number itself causes damage. Lowering BP with drugs reduces stroke, heart attack, heart failure and kidney decline [literature, A].
 - His claims that BP drugs are "biochemically backwards" and that hypertension doesn't damage kidneys are **contradicted by the evidence**.
 - His message that "you're not eating enough salt" [his framing] conflicts with **grade A** evidence for cutting sodium in hypertension.
- **Treat the cause AND the number.**

### Mechanism chain
1. **Pressure = flow × resistance (P = Q × R).** He teaches this himself [his framing]. Resistance is the width of the garden hose.
2. **Endothelial eNOS makes nitric oxide, which relaxes the vessel.** NO is the "open wide" message. Insulin resistance and oxidative stress uncouple eNOS, so the message gets garbled.
3. **High insulin makes the kidney's proximal tubule reabsorb more sodium, so blood volume rises.** More water in a closed system.
4. **The sympathetic nervous system releases norepinephrine onto α1 receptors.** A foot pressing the clamp on the hose [his framing + literature].
5. **RAAS: renin → angiotensin II (constricts vessels, signals aldosterone to retain sodium).** He calls it the kidney's alarm system "at max overdrive" [his framing].
6. **Chronic pressure causes arterial stiffening, a thicker left ventricle and kidney arteriole damage.** The hose turns brittle and the pump bulks up [literature].

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Measure correctly:** home BP with a validated upper-arm cuff, seated after 5 min rest, 2 readings morning and evening for 7 days. Average them, ignoring day 1.
- **Sodium:** below 2,000 mg/day, about 5 g of salt (WHO). The AHA ideal is below 1,500 mg/day. **A.** In practice this means cutting processed and restaurant foods, bread, cheese and deli meats, which supply about 70% of intake.
- **Potassium from food:** 3,500–4,700 mg/day from vegetables, fruit, legumes and dairy (DASH). Get potassium from food, not supplements, and check with the physician first if you have CKD or take an ACEi/ARB or spironolactone.
- **DASH eating pattern:** **A**, about 8–11 mmHg lower systolic in people with hypertension.
- **Aerobic exercise:** 150 min/week (about 5–8 mmHg lower).
- **Isometric training:** wall sit or handgrip, 4 × 2 min, 3×/week. Meta-analyses rank it among the most effective exercise types for lowering BP [literature, A/B]. Breathe normally; no breath-holding.
- **Weight:** about 1 mmHg lower per kg lost if overweight.
- **Alcohol:** ≤1 drink/day; less is better.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. **Get screened for sleep apnea**, a common cause of resistant hypertension.
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **Hydration:** drink to thirst; pale-yellow urine.

- **Thiazide-like diuretics (chlorthalidone, indapamide).** Sodium loss plus vessel relaxation. **A**.

- **Beta-blockers.** Block sympathetic β1 receptors. **A** for specific indications (after a heart attack, heart failure, AFib). He criticizes them [his framing]; their place is indication-specific.

## 5. Kidney disease (CKD)

### Hook
- **EN:** "Kidney disease is a metabolic disease that's wearing a kidney costume" [his framing]. Take off the costume, but keep the medicines that protect the organ underneath.
- **HE:** מחלת כליות היא מחלה מטבולית שמתחפשת לכליות. מורידים את התחפושת, אבל לא זורקים את מה שמגן על הכליה.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fuel is primary [his framing]:** insulin resistance → glomerular hyperfiltration, AGE glycation of the basement membrane, tubular "energy crisis."
- **Fire is the accelerant [his framing]:** TNF-α/IL-6 → adhesion molecules → podocyte injury → proteinuria → fibrosis, "the final common pathway."
- **Power:** proximal tubule cells are among the most mitochondria-dense in the body. When they fail, acute kidney injury progresses to CKD [his framing; mitochondrial density is literature-supported].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - "Hypertension does not cause kidney disease, period" is **contradicted**; hypertensive nephrosclerosis is well documented.
 - ACEi/ARBs and statins **slow** CKD; they are not harmful (RENAAL, IDNT, SHARP).
 - KDIGO does **not** recommend high protein in CKD. The 2024 guideline is 0.8 g/kg/day for stages G3–G5 not on dialysis.
 - "Fire your doctor" and "dialysis is a business": **do not inherit**.
 - Damage reversal: early albuminuria can regress, but established glomerulosclerosis generally does not. Avoid cure promises.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Hyperinsulinemia and hyperglycemia → afferent arteriole dilation → glomerular hyperfiltration.** The filter runs on high pressure 24/7.
2. **Glucose glycates collagen IV and laminin in the glomerular basement membrane, forming AGEs that activate RAGE.** The filter mesh stiffens like sugar-glazed fabric [his framing + literature].
3. **Podocyte foot processes retract and albumin leaks (UACR rises).** The octopus arms holding the filter let go [his framing].
4. **Leaked proteins inflame the tubules via NLRP3, IL-1β and TGF-β.** Spilled cargo poisons the downstream workers.
5. **The epithelium transitions to fibroblasts and fibrosis follows; nephrons are lost.** The factory gets bricked over.
6. **The remaining nephrons hyperfilter to compensate, and the cycle accelerates.** Fewer workers, each on double shifts. SGLT2 inhibitors and RAAS blockers lower that pressure, which is why they work [literature].

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Protein:** 0.8 g/kg/day for CKD stages G3–G5 not on dialysis (KDIGO 2024). Avoid above 1.3 g/kg/day if progression risk is high. Plant-forward sources. Higher targets on dialysis are set by the renal dietitian. **His "crank up the protein" conflicts with the guideline.**
- **Sodium:** below 2,000 mg/day, about 5 g of salt (KDIGO). His 5–10 g of salt per day contradicts this; don't follow it.
- **Potassium and phosphate:** follow your labs and the renal dietitian. Not a DIY area.
- **Fluids:** drink to thirst; no forced high-volume intake unless prescribed (for example for kidney stones).
- **Movement:** 150 min/week moderate, plus 2×/week resistance training. 7,000–10,000 steps/day as tolerated.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h. No prolonged fasting on glucose-lowering drugs or diuretics without the physician (risk of hypoglycemia and acute kidney injury).
- **Glucose:** individual HbA1c target with your physician.
- **Smoking:** zero.
- **Avoid nephrotoxins:** NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen), unprescribed herbal products (aristolochic acid contamination), high-dose vitamin C (oxalate). Have contrast scans and new drugs dose-checked for kidney function.
- **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight.

## 6. Thyroid: low T3, poor conversion, Hashimoto's

### Hook
- **EN:** Your gland can make plenty of T4 while your cells starve for T3. The factory isn't the only problem; the supply chain is.
- **HE:** הבלוטה יכולה לייצר מספיק, והתאים עדיין יגוועו ל-T3. הבעיה היא לא רק במפעל, היא בשרשרת האספקה.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fire is primary for Hashimoto's.** He attributes autoimmune thyroiditis to gut dysbiosis [his framing]. Inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α) suppress TRH and deiodinase [his framing + literature].
- **Fuel:** insulin resistance and chronic dieting reduce T4→T3 conversion. He says low T3 means "fewer glucagon receptors," which blunts drugs like retatrutide [his framing]. Calorie restriction lowering T3 is well documented [literature].
- **Power:** T3 sets mitochondrial number and metabolic rate, so low T3 means less ATP. Hypothyroidism is a classic mitochondrial slowdown [literature].
- **The spiral:** inflammation → less conversion → low energy and cold intolerance → less activity → worse insulin sensitivity → more inflammation.
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - He calls TSH a "bunky, worthless number" because "TSH is downstream." Guidelines use TSH as the **primary** screening and monitoring test. Keep it, and add free T4, free T3 and antibodies.
 - His thyroid targets are his own and not consensus: free T3 "in the upper third," free T4 in the upper half, rT3 below 15.
 - A short strict carnivore protocol for positive antibodies [his framing] has no trial evidence.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Hypothalamic TRH → pituitary TSH → thyroid TPO enzyme iodinates thyroglobulin to make T4.** HQ, middle manager, factory.
2. **Deiodinase 1 and 2 convert T4 into active T3 in tissues; both are selenium-dependent.** T4 is the shipping container; T3 is the unpacked product. Selenium is the box cutter.
3. **Deiodinase 3 converts T4 into reverse T3, which is inactive.** A decoy product that clogs the shelf. It rises in illness and calorie restriction [literature].
4. **T3 binds nuclear thyroid receptors and switches on mitochondrial biogenesis (PGC-1α) and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase.** It turns the thermostat up across the whole building.
5. **In Hashimoto's, autoreactive T cells and anti-TPO/anti-Tg antibodies destroy thyroid tissue.** Friendly fire on the factory. Regulatory T-cell failure is the breakdown in immune tolerance [literature].
6. **As the gland fails, TSH rises to push the remaining tissue.** The manager yells louder at fewer workers. That's why TSH matters.

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Calories:** avoid prolonged aggressive deficits; aim for at most ~0.5–1% of body weight lost per week.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day. If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Carbohydrates:** don't go ultra-low long-term if T3 is falling. Include whole-food carbohydrates (rice, potatoes, fruit) around training [inferred from his "white rice" framing plus literature on low-carb diets lowering T3].
- **Iodine:** normal dietary intake. **Avoid kelp and iodine megadose supplements in Hashimoto's**; excess iodine can worsen autoimmune thyroiditis [literature].
- **Gluten:** a gluten-free diet only if celiac disease is confirmed (tTG-IgA with total IgA). Celiac is more common alongside Hashimoto's [literature].
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight.
- **Training:** strength 2–3×/week plus 150 min/week aerobic. **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **If on levothyroxine:** absorption is affected by food, coffee, calcium, iron, magnesium and PPIs. Follow your prescriber's or pharmacist's timing instructions exactly and keep them consistent.
- **Cut:** alcohol, smoking. Manage stress (cortisol suppresses TSH and conversion).

### Close
- **EN:** TSH is the manager, T3 is the worker. Watch both and feed the supply chain.

## 7. Frequent infections, herpes/EBV reactivation, immune resilience

### Hook
- **EN:** The virus doesn't come back because it's strong. It comes back because your guards are exhausted. Rebuild the guard, don't just bomb the intruder.
- **HE:** הנגיף לא חוזר כי הוא חזק. הוא חוזר כי השומרים שלך עייפים.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fire, misdirected [his framing].** Herpes "isn't a skin infection"; it's a neurological virus hiding in sensory ganglia. He calls EBV "a mole inside your immune system" that hijacks B cells and exhausts T cells. Chronic, misdirected inflammation leaves the immune system loud and ineffective. His EBV chapter title: "rebuild don't boost."
- **Power:** activated immune cells are energetically expensive. Low ATP impairs regulatory T cells and leads to "a dysregulated immune system" [his framing].
- **Fuel:** hyperglycemia impairs neutrophil function. Diabetes raises infection risk, including shingles [literature]. He blames sugar and diet for outbreaks [his framing].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - Antivirals (acyclovir, valacyclovir) are **effective**: suppressive therapy cuts herpes recurrences by about 70–80% and reduces transmission [literature, A].
 - Antivirals are kidney-cleared, so dosing is adjusted for kidney function, but "kidney and neurologic issues" are uncommon at standard doses.
 - A positive EBV IgG is normal in ~90–95% of adults. It means a past infection, not "active EBV."

### Mechanism chain
1. **HSV establishes latency in sensory-nerve ganglia and expresses only LAT transcripts.** The virus sleeps in the nerve's basement with the lights off.
2. **Stress, UV, fever, illness and hormonal shifts reduce local CD8⁺ T-cell surveillance → reactivation → the virus travels down the axon.** The night guard dozes off and the intruder slips out the service door.
3. **EBV latently infects memory B cells (EBNA proteins).** A spy with a valid employee badge [his "mole" framing].
4. **Chronic antigen exposure → T-cell exhaustion (PD-1⁺).** Soldiers on endless shifts stop shooting straight.
5. **The thymus involutes with age (less GH, more stress hormones) → fewer naïve T cells.** He describes the thymus as "boot camp for your t cells" [his framing]. Boot camp downsizes with age [literature].
6. **Sleep loss, high glucose, and deficient vitamin D or zinc impair interferon and NK responses.** Guards without food, sleep or ammunition.

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Sleeping under 6 h was linked to about 4× higher risk of catching a cold after a controlled rhinovirus exposure [literature].
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (antibodies and immune cells are protein). If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Exercise:** moderate, 150–300 min/week, plus 2×/week strength. Avoid chronic over-reaching without recovery.
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day.
- **Glucose:** cut sugary drinks and refined snacks; get HbA1c checked.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight. **For herpes labialis:** SPF 30+ lip balm in strong sun or snow; UV is a classic trigger.
- **Stress:** a daily 10-min down-regulation practice (slow breathing, walk).
- **Alcohol:** ≤2 drinks/week. **Smoking:** zero.
- **Vaccinations:** shingles (recombinant zoster vaccine, age ≥50 or immunocompromised), influenza, COVID-19 and pneumococcal per Israeli Ministry of Health schedule. **A.**

## 8. Autoimmunity (general)

### Hook
- **EN:** Autoimmunity isn't an immune system that's too strong. It's a badly trained one that was never told to stand down.
- **HE:** אוטואימוניות זו לא מערכת חיסון חזקה מדי. זו מערכת חיסון שאף אחד לא אמר לה "מספיק".

### Big-Three routing
- **Fire is primary [his framing].** "A dysregulated immune system" with persistent NF-κB. He frames the goal as resolving inflammation rather than suppressing it: telling the immune system the battle is over and it can stand down [paraphrase of his BPC framing]. He calls the three-failures framing of all chronic disease, autoimmunity included, "100% my theory" [his framing, per audit].
- **Power:** low ATP impairs regulatory T cells; mitochondrial ROS and leaked mitochondrial DNA activate innate immunity (TLRs) [his framing + literature: mtDNA/TLR9 and cGAS-STING are real pathways].
- **Fuel:** obesity and insulin resistance are pro-inflammatory (adipokines, IL-6) and are risk factors for several autoimmune diseases [literature].
- **Gut barrier:** he links zonulin → tight-junction opening → endotoxemia → immune attack (celiac, Crohn's, eczema) [his framing].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**
 - Autoimmunity does **not** reduce cleanly to three failures. Genetics (HLA), sex hormones, infections and environmental triggers matter [literature].
 - Immunosuppressants and DMARDs prevent organ damage and disability. **Never stop them yourself.**

### Mechanism chain
1. **Loss of tolerance: autoreactive T cells escape thymic deletion, and Treg (FOXP3⁺) function falls.** The referees left the field.
2. **An antigen-presenting cell shows self-antigen with co-stimulation → Th1 / Th17 activation.** Wrong mugshots posted on the wanted wall.
3. **IL-17, TNF-α, IL-6 and interferons → NF-κB keeps the inflammatory genes switched on.** The fire alarm is wired to a sprinkler that sprays gasoline.
4. **B cells make autoantibodies → immune complexes, complement, tissue injury.** Mislabelled missiles.
5. **Barrier leak (tight junctions) → microbial products (LPS) reach the blood → TLR4 activation.** The fence is down and the alarms keep tripping [his framing + literature, association-level].
6. **Resolution failure: low specialized pro-resolving mediators (resolvins, from EPA/DHA).** Nobody calls "all clear," so the crews never go home [literature].

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Smoking:** zero. A major modifiable risk factor for RA, lupus flares and Graves' eye disease. **A.**
- **Diet:** Mediterranean pattern. Oily fish 2–3×/week. ≥30 g fibre/day (feeds butyrate-producing bacteria). Minimize ultra-processed food.
- **Elimination diets:** only with a clinician's guidance and a clear diagnosis (for example gluten-free for confirmed celiac).
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (more during steroid therapy to protect muscle). If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD (including lupus nephritis), follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h; sleep loss raises IL-6.
- **Training:** 150 min/week moderate aerobic plus 2×/week strength, scaled to flares. Exercise lowers inflammatory markers in RA and lupus without worsening disease [literature].
- **Steps:** 6,000–10,000/day as tolerated.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h.
- **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight. **Photosensitive conditions such as lupus need sun protection.**
- **Weight:** if obese, 5–10% loss lowers disease activity in psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis and improves drug response [literature].
- **Alcohol:** minimal, especially on methotrexate (liver).

### Big-Three routing
- **Power outage [his store copy + inferred]:** his store frames its alcohol stack around "mitochondrial energy failure + neuronal damage" [his store copy]. Ethanol metabolism floods the liver with NADH, collapsing the NAD⁺/NADH ratio, which stalls fat oxidation [literature].

- **Fire:** gut-barrier leak → LPS reaches the liver's Kupffer cells (his phrase: "the bouncer at the liver") → TNF-α → alcoholic hepatitis [literature + his metaphor].
- **The spiral:** drinking → leaky gut → liver inflammation → fatty liver → insulin resistance → poor sleep and cravings → more drinking.
- **On labs:** ALT is "the smoke alarm that goes off after three-quarters of your house is already charred"; "nobody checks GGT" [his framing].
- **Where the literature corrects him:**

### Mechanism chain
1. **Alcohol dehydrogenase converts ethanol to acetaldehyde, consuming NAD⁺ and producing NADH.** The liver uses up its rechargeable batteries just to process the drink.
2. **Acetaldehyde forms protein and DNA adducts.** Acetaldehyde from alcohol consumption is an IARC Group 1 carcinogen; it welds itself to cellular machinery [literature].
3. **CYP2E1 induction produces ROS and depletes glutathione.** Chronic drinking builds a second, dirtier incinerator that smokes [literature]. GGT rises as part of the glutathione and oxidative-stress response.
4. **A high NADH/NAD⁺ ratio blocks β-oxidation and drives new fat production (via SREBP-1c) → fatty liver.** The furnace is off but the fat delivery trucks keep arriving.
5. **Gut tight junctions loosen → LPS → TLR4 on Kupffer cells → TNF-α → hepatocyte injury → stellate cells lay down fibrosis.** The bouncer starts brawling with the staff, then the scar contractors move in.
6. **Thiamine depletion and impaired brain glucose metabolism → neuronal energy failure (Wernicke–Korsakoff in severe cases).** The brain's power plant runs out of a single critical spark plug [literature].

### Tier 0 foundations
- **Alcohol target:** zero during recovery. If you drink heavily every day, **do not stop abruptly without medical advice** (withdrawal seizures, delirium tremens). Use a supervised plan.
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day. Malnutrition is common; the liver needs protein to rebuild. Do not restrict protein in liver disease unless a hepatologist says so. If eGFR is below 60 or you have known CKD, follow Problem 5 (0.8 g/kg/day) and your nephrologist.
- **Late-evening snack:** if cirrhosis is present, a protein/carbohydrate snack before bed (guideline practice) to avoid overnight muscle breakdown.
- **Cut:** fructose (sugary drinks, juices, sweets), ultra-processed food.
- **Coffee:** 2–3 cups/day is associated with lower liver fibrosis risk [literature, C/observational], if tolerated and without arrhythmia.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Expect sleep to be disrupted for weeks after stopping; that's part of recovery.
- **Light:** 10–20 min of morning daylight to re-anchor the circadian rhythm.
- **Training:** 150 min/week aerobic plus 2×/week strength (reduces liver fat independently of weight loss).
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000/day.
- **Eating window:** 10–12 h. Avoid prolonged fasting if malnourished or cirrhotic.
- **Hydration:** water to thirst. Electrolytes through food.
- **Support:** counselling, peer groups, or a structured program. Behavioral treatment is **A**.

- **Liver workup and staging** (FIB-4, elastography, hepatology referral). **A**.
