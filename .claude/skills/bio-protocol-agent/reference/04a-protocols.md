# PROTOCOL LIBRARY, Part 1
### Metabolic, energy and brain problems, routed through the "three biological failures" framework

---

## How to read this library

**Provenance tags**
- **[his framing]** is how Trevor Bachmeyer explains or routes the problem, taken from his videos and posts. Text in quotation marks was checked against the raw transcript corpus. Anything that could not be found there is marked **[paraphrase]**.
- **[literature]** is what the independent evidence supports.
- **[inferred]** means he has no protocol for this point in the corpus, so it was built from his framework, or it is our own synthesis.

**Evidence grades**
- **A:** human RCTs or meta-analyses for this indication.
- **B:** small RCTs, or trials that only measured surrogate endpoints.
- **C:** observational data, or small trials that disagree with each other.
- **D:** animal or in-vitro data only.

**The three failures [his framing]**
- **Fire:** systemic inflammation.
- **Fuel:** insulin resistance.
- **Power outage:** mitochondrial dysfunction, which he calls "ATP bankruptcy."

He says they feed each other in a "death spiral." As a teaching device this is reasonable: these are real, measurable and connected processes [literature]. What the evidence does *not* support is the jump from "this compound touches one of those processes in a rodent" to "this compound treats your disease" [literature].

**Hard boundaries in this document**
1. **No doses for peptides, research compounds, prescription drugs or hormones.** No routes, frequencies, cycles or titration. Not even attributed to him.
2. **Supplement doses stay within the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL)** and within the range used in human trials. Each one carries a grade.
3. **Every if/then rule ends in a safe action:** stop, retest, or see the physician.
4. **Nothing here replaces prescribed treatment. Never stop a prescribed drug on your own.**

**Regulatory snapshot, verified for this library (October 2026)**
- **FDA bulk-substances list.** These sit in the "nominated then withdrawn" table, which keeps FDA's safety language: BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon, injectable GHK-Cu, Thymosin α-1, CJC-1295, DSIP, Dihexa, KPV, Selank and Semax.
 - Kisspeptin-10 (503A) and Ipamorelin (503B) are on the **active** Category 2 list.
 - SS-31, methylene blue, NAD+ and retatrutide are not on that page.
- **WADA 2026 Prohibited List.**
 - BPC-157: S0.
 - TB-500: S2.3.
 - MOTS-c: S4.4.1 (AMPK activators).
 - Tesamorelin, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin: S2.2.4.
 - Kisspeptin: S2.2.1.
 - Non-approved GLP-1-type agents such as retatrutide fall under the S0 catch-all.
- **Retatrutide.** Still investigational and not approved anywhere.
 - Phase 3 TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 were positive (July 2026).
 - Lilly plans to file in Q1 2027 ([HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/retatrutide-meets-phase-3-weight-loss-endpoints-in-2-obesity-trials), [Epocrates](https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/retatrutide-heads-toward-fda-filing-after-positive-phase-3-obesity-trial-results)).
 - Anything sold as "retatrutide" today is a research chemical of unverified identity. Seven Australians were hospitalised with acute liver toxicity after taking products labelled retatrutide.
- **Orforglipron (Foundayo).** An oral GLP-1 agonist, FDA-approved for obesity on 1 April 2026 ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260401/fda-approves-orforglipron-an-oral-glp1-for-adults-with-obesity)).
- **Israel.**
 - Wegovy and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) are marketed with a prescription ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/s19adqlaa)).
 - Israeli registration of the research peptides could not be verified; the registry was down during research. Treat them as **not registered**.
- **Labs in Israel [inferred, confirm with your health fund].**
 - Usually available through your health fund (HMO): CBC, CMP, lipids, HbA1c, fasting glucose, TSH/fT4, ferritin, B12, vitamin D, urinalysis and eGFR. Fasting insulin, ApoB, hs-CRP and homocysteine usually need a specific request from the physician.
 - Four-point salivary cortisol is usually private.
 - FibroScan (elastography) and sleep studies need a referral.

**On "his optimal" lab targets.** These come from his videos and are tighter than guideline ranges. Treat them as **his** numbers, not consensus. The thing to watch is your *trend* on the same lab, interpreted with a physician.

# 1. Insulin resistance / prediabetes / type 2 diabetes

### Hook
- **EN:** "Your cells don't close the door on glucose because they're lazy. They close the door because they're full." [his framing, verbatim]
- **HE:** התאים שלכם לא טרקו את הדלת לסוכר כי הם עצלנים. הם טרקו אותה כי הם מפוצצים.

### Big-Three routing
- **Primary failure: fuel (insulin resistance).**
 - He calls it "the fuel problem" and says it is downstream of the other two [his framing].
 - In his words: "inflammation is the root, insulin resistance is the manifestation" [his framing, verbatim].
- **Fire.** Inflammatory signals (TNF-α, IL-6, JNK) jam the insulin signal at IRS-1 [his framing]. That mechanism is real and well described in fat and muscle biology [literature].
- **Power outage.** When mitochondria can't oxidise incoming fuel fast enough, fat piles up inside muscle cells. Lipid by-products (DAGs, ceramides) then block insulin signalling [his framing]. This "lipid-induced insulin resistance" idea has strong human support, including Shulman's intramyocellular-lipid work [literature].
- **The spiral** [his framing; directionally supported by literature]:
 1. High insulin turns off fat burning.
 2. Sugar-damaged proteins (AGEs) drive inflammation.
 3. Inflammation and ROS damage mitochondria.
 4. Less fuel is burned, so more fat is stored in the wrong places.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Energy surplus plus low mitochondrial capacity, leading to intramyocellular lipid.** Metaphor: the warehouse is full and the trucks keep arriving [literature + his framing].
2. **DAGs activate PKC-θ, which puts a serine phosphate on IRS-1; ceramides block Akt further downstream.** Metaphor: the key still fits the lock, but someone cut the wire behind it ("cut the wires" [his framing]) [literature].
3. **The pancreas compensates with more insulin (hyperinsulinaemia).** Metaphor: the pancreas yells louder because nobody is listening. Fasting insulin rises *years* before glucose does [literature].
4. **High insulin keeps malonyl-CoA high, which blocks CPT-1.** Metaphor: the door into the mitochondrial furnace is locked, so fat can't be burned and gets stored [literature].
5. **The liver becomes insulin-resistant, so it keeps making glucose and makes new fat (DNL).** Metaphor: the liver has no off switch, so fasting glucose and triglycerides creep up [literature].
6. **Beta-cell exhaustion.** Metaphor: the shouting pancreas finally loses its voice, and HbA1c climbs into the diabetes range [literature].

### Tier 0: foundations
**Sleep**
- Aim for 7–9 h with a fixed wake time, ±30 min, seven days a week.
- One night of sleep restriction measurably lowers insulin sensitivity [literature, A/B].

**Protein**
- 1.2–1.6 g/kg of goal body weight per day, in 3 meals of 25–40 g.
- Exception: in kidney disease (eGFR <60) the nephrologist sets the target.

**Walking**
- 7,000–10,000 steps per day.
- **Walk 10–15 min after each main meal.** This blunts the post-meal glucose spike [literature, B].
- He uses "walk 1 mile… every day at a 16 minute pace" [his framing, verbatim].

**Training**
- Resistance training 2–3×/week, whole body, close to failure.
- 150 min/week of moderate aerobic exercise (ADA guidance) [literature, A].
- Muscle is the biggest glucose sink. He agrees: "Build muscle" as the glucose sink [his framing].

**Eating window**
- Default: an 8–10 h window, finishing 2–3 h before bed.
- He prescribes a strict 18:6 [his framing]. Evidence check: in the real 2022 NEJM trial (Liu et al.), time-restricted eating added **no** benefit over the same calorie restriction [literature, A]. A small trial of early time-restricted eating did improve insulin sensitivity [literature, B].
- **On insulin, a sulfonylurea or an SGLT2 inhibitor? Do not change your fasting pattern without your physician.** The risks are hypoglycaemia and euglycaemic ketoacidosis.

**Diet pattern**
- A Mediterranean-style pattern or a moderately lower-carb pattern both improve HbA1c [literature, A/B]. Pick the one you can sustain.
- He pushes "strategic carnivore" with white rice after training [his framing]. That is not needed, and it is not appropriate for kidney disease.
- **Weight:** losing 5–7% of body weight cut progression to diabetes by 58% in the Diabetes Prevention Program [literature, A]. Losing 10–15 kg put about half of people with early T2D into **remission** in the DiRECT trial [literature, A]. Note the word is "remission," not "cure."

**Light**
- 10–30 min of outdoor light within an hour of waking.

**Cut**
- Sugar-sweetened drinks and fruit juice: zero.
- Added sugar: under 25 g/day.
- Refined starch: replace it.
- Alcohol: none is ideal; at most a few drinks a week.
- Smoking: stop.

### Tier 2: prescription options to raise with a physician
- **Metformin.** Suppresses glucose production by the liver through redox and gut mechanisms [literature]. **A** for T2D. It cut progression in prediabetes in the DPP trial.
 - He calls it "limp mode" for mitochondria [his framing, paraphrase]. The evidence: complex-I inhibition only happens at concentrations far above clinical levels [literature].
 - Real caveats: it depletes B12 (monitor it), and it may blunt aerobic training gains in older adults.
- **GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide) and GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide).** Glucose-dependent insulin release, less glucagon, appetite reduction. **A.**
 - In the obesity-plus-prediabetes cohort over 176 weeks, tirzepatide cut progression to T2D to 1.3% vs 13.3% on placebo [literature].
- **Orforglipron** (oral GLP-1, FDA-approved April 2026 for obesity). **A** for weight loss.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors.** Make the kidney excrete glucose. **A** for cardiovascular and renal outcomes in T2D. Avoid very-low-carb diets while taking them (ketoacidosis risk).
- **Pioglitazone.** A PPAR-γ insulin sensitiser. **A** for glycaemia, **B** for MASH. Risks: fluid retention, heart failure, fractures, weight gain.

**Recheck cadence.** Baseline, then at 12 weeks: HbA1c, fasting glucose and insulin, lipids. After that every 3–6 months. A CGM for 10–14 days is optional and teaches you which meals spike you (**C** for people without diabetes).

**If/then rules (each ends in a safe action)**
- **HbA1c ≥6.5% or fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL** → book a diagnostic visit with the physician now. Do not "self-protocol" first.
- **On glucose-lowering drugs and a reading <70 mg/dL, or shakes/sweats/confusion** → take 15 g of fast carbohydrate, recheck in 15 min, then call the prescriber the **same day**. **Do not adjust the medication yourself.**
- **Starting berberine, or a large diet change, while on glucose-lowering medication** → tell the prescriber *before* you start.
- **No HOMA-IR or HbA1c improvement at 12 weeks despite real adherence** → retest, then review Tier 2 with the physician.
- **Urine albumin rising or eGFR falling** → physician, and nephrology referral.

### Checkpoints
- **Week 2:** check post-meal walks and sleep regularity. Optionally look at CGM spikes and which meals cause them. Weight is not the metric yet.
- **Week 4:** waist circumference and morning energy. Are you training 2–3×/week? Is protein on target?
- **Week 8:** waist and weight trend (aim for 0.5–1% of body weight per week if you need to lose). Home BP.
- **Week 12:** repeat labs (HbA1c, fasting insulin/glucose, HOMA-IR, lipids, ALT). Decide on next steps *with* the physician.

### Red flags: stop and see a doctor
- Excessive thirst or urination, unexplained weight loss, or blurred vision.
- Glucose >250 mg/dL, or ketones.
- Hypoglycaemia episodes.
- Foot wounds, numbness or burning.
- Chest pain or shortness of breath on exertion.
- Nausea with vomiting and abdominal pain while on an SGLT2 inhibitor (possible DKA, even with normal glucose).

### Close
- **EN:** "The variable is you." [his framing, verbatim] Test it, walk it off after dinner, and give your biology 90 days to show you.
- **HE:** המשתנה זה אתם. בדיקה ביד, הליכה אחרי האוכל, ותנו לגוף 90 יום להוכיח.

# 2. Fatty liver (MASLD/NAFLD) and fructose damage

### Hook
- **EN:** Comparing an apple to a Coke is "metabolically incompetent." [his framing, verbatim] Whole fruit isn't the enemy. Liquid fructose is the arsonist.
- **HE:** תפוח וקולה זה לא אותו פרוקטוז. אחד מגיע עם סיבים. השני נוחת ישר על הכבד — עם פטיש.

### Big-Three routing
- **Primary failure: fuel.** The fatty liver is both cause and consequence of insulin resistance.
 - He frames NAFLD as a fructose problem, not an exercise, weight or glucose problem [his framing, paraphrase].
 - Evidence check: fructose is a strong contributor, but calorie surplus, insulin resistance, genetics (PNPLA3) and alcohol all matter [literature].
- **Fire.** Free fatty acids and lipotoxic by-products activate Kupffer cells (TLR4/NF-κB). This drives MASH and then fibrosis [his framing + literature].
- **Power outage.** Mitochondrial overload in liver cells leads to ROS. Fructokinase burns ATP, and the breakdown products raise uric acid [his framing + literature, B/D].
- **The spiral:**
 1. A fatty liver becomes insulin-resistant.
 2. High insulin drives more new fat production (DNL).
 3. More fat means more inflammation, then fibrosis.
- **His special warning** [his framing]: very rapid fat loss on high-dose incretins can overload the liver ("rapid weight loss induced hepatic steatosis" is his own coinage).
 - Evidence check: transient worsening with very-low-calorie diets is described.
 - But the retatrutide phase 2 trial **cut** liver fat by about 80% at the higher doses [literature, B].

### Mechanism chain
1. **Fructose goes to the liver through GLUT2 and is phosphorylated by fructokinase (KHK), which has no feedback brake.** Metaphor: an on-ramp with no traffic light [literature].
2. **ATP is burned, AMP is broken down, and uric acid rises.** Metaphor: the liver's cash register is drained [literature, B/D].
3. **The products (DHAP, glyceraldehyde) feed new fat production through SREBP-1c and ChREBP.** Metaphor: sugar gets turned into fat on site [literature].
4. **Triglyceride production exceeds what VLDL/ApoB can export.** Metaphor: the trucks are full and fat piles up in the warehouse [literature; his "protein suitcase" image].
5. **Hepatic insulin resistance follows (DAG → PKCε → insulin receptor).** Metaphor: the liver stops hearing "stop making sugar" [literature].
6. **Lipotoxicity and ROS activate Kupffer cells, then stellate cells lay down collagen.** Metaphor: scar tissue paves over the factory floor. That is fibrosis, the stage that predicts outcomes [literature, A].

### Tier 0: foundations
**Weight**
- Losing ≥5% of body weight lowers liver fat.
- ≥10% loss was associated with MASH resolution in ~90% and fibrosis regression in ~45% (prospective cohort) [literature, C].
- Pace: about **0.5–1% of body weight per week**, roughly ≤1 kg/week. Losing more than ~1.5 kg/week raises gallstone risk [literature].

**Fructose**
- Sugar-sweetened drinks, juice, energy drinks and sweetened coffee: **zero**.
- Added sugar: under 25 g/day.
- 2–3 portions of **whole** fruit per day are fine [literature; matches his apple-vs-Coke point].

**Alcohol**
- **Zero** if there is any fibrosis.
- Otherwise minimal. MetALD (metabolic plus alcohol liver disease) progresses faster.

**Exercise**
- 150–240 min/week of moderate aerobic exercise, *or* 75–150 min vigorous. This lowers liver fat even without weight loss [literature, A/B].
- Plus resistance training 2–3×/week.

**Other foundations**
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg of goal body weight. This preserves muscle and the liver needs amino acids to build ApoB [literature + his framing].
- **Diet pattern:** Mediterranean is the best-evidenced pattern [literature, B].
- **Fat:** keep healthy fats in. **Don't go fat-free during weight loss**, because some fat in each meal keeps the gallbladder emptying [literature, B; his framing agrees].
- **Coffee:** 2–3 cups/day, black or nearly black. Associated with less fibrosis [literature, C].
- **Sleep 7–9 h; daily walk;** 10 min after the main meal.

**TUDCA** is his gallbladder and bile protector [his framing]. Evidence: **C/D** as a supplement. Its prescription cousin **ursodiol** has the real gallstone-prevention data during rapid weight loss (Tier 2).

### Tier 2: prescription options to raise with a physician
- **Resmetirom (Rezdiffra).** A liver-directed thyroid hormone receptor-β agonist that increases liver fat burning. **A**: approved for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis.
- **Semaglutide.** GLP-1 agonist. **A** (accelerated approval for non-cirrhotic MASH F2–F3; ESSENCE trial).
- **Tirzepatide.** **B** (phase 2 SYNERGY-NASH: MASH resolution).
- **Pioglitazone.** **A/B** for MASH histology. Risks: weight gain, fluid retention, heart failure.
- **Ursodiol.** **A** for preventing gallstones during rapid weight loss, mainly after bariatric surgery.
- **Statins.** Safe in MASLD and indicated for cardiovascular risk (**A** for cardiovascular benefit). Cardiovascular disease is the **#1 cause of death in MASLD** [literature, C (observational)].
- **Retatrutide** (investigational). **B**: liver fat −81 to −82% at 24 weeks in a phase 2 substudy. Not approved (see Tier 3).

**Recheck cadence.** Liver enzymes, lipids and HbA1c at 12 weeks, then every 6 months. FIB-4 every 1–2 years if low risk. Elastography per the hepatologist.

**If/then rules**
- **FIB-4 ≥1.3, or a stiff liver on elastography** → hepatology referral. Do not wait out a 6-month "protocol."
- **ALT or AST >3× ULN, or rising on two tests** → stop any new supplement or compound and see the physician within days.
- **Ferritin >300 with transferrin saturation >45%** → physician (iron-overload work-up).
- **On a GLP-1-class drug, with liver enzymes or GGT rising** → retest and take the result to the prescriber. **The prescriber decides about the dose.**

### Checkpoints
- **Week 2:** sugary drinks fully gone? Alcohol at zero or minimal? Steps and walks logged?
- **Week 4:** waist down? Weight trend about 0.5–1%/week if you need to lose?
- **Week 8:** waist and weight. Any right-upper-quadrant pain after fatty meals? That pain is a gallbladder check, not a "detox."
- **Week 12:** repeat ALT/AST/GGT, lipids, HbA1c and FIB-4. Ask about elastography if not done.

### Red flags: stop and see a doctor
- Yellow eyes or skin.
- Dark urine with pale stools.
- Swollen belly or legs.
- Vomiting blood or black stools.
- Confusion or sleepiness (hepatic encephalopathy).
- Severe right-upper-quadrant pain with fever (gallbladder).
- Severe upper abdominal pain radiating to the back (pancreatitis).
- Easy bruising.

# 3. Fat loss and GLP-1 use (muscle preservation, liver/pancreas monitoring, nausea, anxiety)

### Hook
- **EN:** "It's not a replacement for discipline, it's an amplifier of discipline." [his framing, verbatim] The shot amplifies whatever you're already doing, good or bad.
- **HE:** הזריקה לא עושה את העבודה במקומכם. היא מגבירה את מה שאתם כבר עושים — לטוב ולרע.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fuel.** Obesity is mostly an energy-balance and insulin-resistance problem. Incretins fix "the control panel" (appetite and glucose sensing) [his framing].
- **Power outage.** He says a "broken power plant" blunts the drug response: "Fix the engine before expecting any performance" [his framing, verbatim].
 - Evidence check: there is **no** human evidence that mitochondrial pre-loading improves GLP-1 response [literature, D].
- **Fire.** Visceral fat is inflammatory. Weight loss lowers CRP and IL-6 [literature, A].
- **His spiral of failure on GLP-1s** [his framing]:
 - Too-fast loss with too little protein leads to muscle loss.
 - Muscle loss lowers glucose disposal and metabolic rate.
 - Glycogen, water and electrolyte loss drive fatigue and anxiety.
 - He calls it "a usage issue, it's not a peptide issue" [verbatim].

### Mechanism chain
1. **GLP-1 receptors in the hypothalamus and brainstem (arcuate POMC↑/AgRP↓, area postrema).** Metaphor: the appetite volume knob is turned down. The same site causes nausea [literature].
2. **Slower gastric emptying.** Metaphor: the stomach holds food longer, so you feel full. This is also the source of nausea, reflux and aspiration risk under anaesthesia [literature].
3. **Glucose-dependent insulin release and less glucagon.** Metaphor: the pancreas speaks only when there's sugar to answer [literature].
4. **Energy deficit, then fat *and* lean-mass loss.** On tirzepatide, about 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% lean mass, the **same proportion as with dieting alone** [literature, A].
 - He says GLP-1s put muscle in an "energy scarcity" signal, so "more protein won't help" [verbatim, caption]. The evidence check: protein plus resistance training **is** the evidence-based defence [literature, A/B]. BCAA-only strategies are unproven (D).
5. **Rapid loss mobilises cholesterol into bile while the gallbladder empties more slowly.** Metaphor: concentrated bile sitting still forms sludge and then stones [literature, A].
6. **Low intake plus fluid and electrolyte loss.** Metaphor: running the engine on fumes. The results are dizziness, palpitations, fatigue, anxiety-like symptoms, and an acute kidney injury risk with vomiting or diarrhoea [literature].

### Tier 0: foundations
**Protein (the #1 muscle lever)**
- 1.2–1.6 g/kg of goal body weight per day.
- Hit **25–40 g per meal, eaten first.** That gives about 2.5–3 g of leucine from whole protein.
- Use a whey or other protein shake if your appetite is gone.
- Exception: CKD, where the nephrologist sets protein.

**Resistance training**
- 2–3×/week, all major muscle groups, progressive load. **Non-negotiable** for preserving lean mass [literature, A/B].

**Walking**
- 7,000–10,000 steps/day; 10 min after meals.

**Rate of loss**
- About **0.5–1% of body weight per week** (roughly 0.5–1 kg/week). This protects muscle and lowers gallstone risk [literature]. He targets about 1–1.2%/week [his framing], which points the same way.

**Eating pattern**
- 3 structured meals, protein first, vegetables second.
- **Don't drop to one meal a day by accident**, and don't run a deficit deeper than about 500–750 kcal/day without supervision.
- Keep some fat in every meal for the gallbladder.

**Hydration**
- About 30–35 mL/kg/day *on a schedule, not by thirst*, because GLP-1s blunt thirst. He makes the same point [his framing]. Pale-yellow urine is the target.
- **Exception: heart or kidney fluid restriction**, where the physician sets the volume.

**Carbohydrates**
- He insists retatrutide users should *not* go keto: he says incretin action is glucose-dependent and muscle needs glycogen [his framing].
- A moderate carb intake (mostly whole-food) is reasonable. No extreme diet is required.

**Alcohol**
- Minimal. It adds empty calories and worsens nausea and hypoglycaemia risk.

**Nausea management**
- Small meals; stop at the first sign of fullness.
- Avoid greasy, fried or very sweet food.
- Don't lie down for 2–3 h after eating.
- Sip fluids between meals rather than during them.

**Anxiety on a GLP-1**
- First check the mundane causes: low intake, dehydration, too much caffeine on an empty stomach, poor sleep, hypoglycaemia (if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea).
- He calls it "a usage issue, it's not a peptide issue" [his framing, verbatim].
- What the evidence says: large regulator reviews (FDA 2024, EMA 2024) found **no causal link** between GLP-1 agonists and suicidal thoughts, but labels still advise monitoring mood [literature].

# 4. Chronic fatigue / waking exhausted ("ATP bankruptcy")

### Hook
- **EN:** "You're not tired because you're old. You're tired because you're energetically bankrupt." [his framing, verbatim]
- **HE:** אתם לא עייפים כי אתם מבוגרים. אתם עייפים כי התאים שלכם פשטו רגל.

### Big-Three routing
- **Primary failure: power outage.** "This ain't a rest problem, sweetheart. This is a building problem." [his framing, verbatim]
 - He says "A broken power plant doesn't give you a disease… a broken power plant is disease." [verbatim]
- **Fuel.** In his telling, starved cells down-regulate insulin signalling and get hyperinsulinaemia; insulin resistance comes *after* mitochondrial decline [his framing].
- **Fire.** Low ATP starves regulatory T cells and lets M1 macrophages run unchecked. TNF-α and IL-6 then create more ROS, which means less ATP. He calls it the "circle of death" [his framing].
 - Evidence check: the immunometabolism links are real in the lab, but his specific percentages couldn't be verified [literature].
- **Literature view.** Persistent fatigue has a long list of *measurable, treatable* causes, and those come first:
 - anaemia or iron deficiency, hypothyroidism, sleep apnoea, depression, B12 deficiency, diabetes, coeliac disease, and medication effects;
 - ME/CFS, whose hallmark is post-exertional malaise (PEM).
- **The spiral** [his framing]: poor fuel leads to electron leak and ROS, which damage the electron transport chain. That lowers ATP, which shuts down repair and immune regulation and feeds more inflammation.

### Mechanism chain
1. **NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to complexes I–IV, building a proton gradient that drives ATP synthase.** Metaphor: water behind a dam spinning the turbine [literature].
2. **When complexes are damaged or substrate-overloaded, electrons leak and form superoxide (ROS).** Metaphor: sparks flying off a frayed power line [literature].
3. **ROS damage cardiolipin and mtDNA, so fewer efficient mitochondria remain.** Metaphor: the power plant loses turbines [literature, mostly preclinical].
4. **Low ATP is sensed by AMPK; healthy signalling (PGC-1α) builds new mitochondria, while chronic stress just leaves you rationing energy.** Metaphor: a low-battery alarm that either triggers a rebuild or a blackout [literature].
5. **Inflammatory cytokines cause "sickness behaviour" (fatigue, low motivation) through the brain.** Metaphor: the body's "stay in bed" order [literature, A for the mechanism in humans].
6. **Exercise and good sleep are the strongest proven triggers for building new mitochondria.** Metaphor: you build more turbines by using them, unless the system is in PEM [literature, A].

### Tier 0: foundations
**First, screen for post-exertional malaise (PEM).**
- If exertion makes you crash 12–72 h *later*, you need **pacing**, not pushing.
- In that case, see a physician and do not follow the training targets below.

**Sleep**
- Fixed wake time; 7–9 h in bed.
- Screen for apnoea (snoring, witnessed pauses, morning headache, high blood pressure; use STOP-BANG).
- "Sleep is a repair window" [his framing, verbatim].

**Light**
- 10–30 min outdoors within an hour of waking.

**Movement (if no PEM)**
- Daily walk building toward 7,000+ steps.
- Resistance training 2×/week; add aerobic work gradually.

**Protein**
- 1.2 g/kg/day across 3 meals.

**Eating window**
- Regular meals within a 10–12 h window.
- No fasting experiments while exhausted. He says he never starts fasted when adapting [his framing].

**Caffeine**
- ≤400 mg/day (≤200 mg in pregnancy), all before noon.
- He keeps coffee but calls stimulants used for energy masking [his framing, paraphrase].

**Alcohol**
- None, or very little. It fragments sleep.

**What he says to remove** [his framing]
- Night-time diphenhydramine (Benadryl/ZzzQuil). This is legitimate: it is on the Beers list of drugs to avoid in older adults [literature].
- Energy drinks.
- Ultra-processed food.

### Hook
- **EN:** "A disease isn't a plumbing problem, it's an energy problem." [his framing, verbatim] Your brain is 2% of your weight and burns about 20% of your energy, so it's the first to dim.
- **HE:** הערפל בראש הוא לא "ככה זה בגיל הזה". זו נפילת מתח — המוח הוא הצרכן הכי רעב בבית, והוא הראשון שמחשיך.

### Big-Three routing
- **The corpus has no dedicated brain-fog protocol.** It has a cognition/memory product page and the dementia and fatigue protocols, so this section is built from his framework [inferred].
- **Power outage.** The brain uses about 20% of resting energy, so a mild ATP shortfall shows up as slow thinking [his framing; the 20% figure is literature].

# 6. Alzheimer's / dementia prevention ("type 3 diabetes")

### Hook
- **EN:** On amyloid: "it's a tombstone." [his framing, verbatim] The war is won or lost 20 years before the first symptom.
- **HE:** העמילואיד הוא לא הרוצח — הוא המצבה. את המלחמה מנצחים עשרים שנה לפני הסימפטום הראשון.

### Big-Three routing
- **All three failures** [his framing]. In his words: "Insulin resistance is the catalyst… the resulting chronic inflammation destroys the mitochondria" [verbatim]. He also says "it's not a neurological disease… it's a metabolic disease period" [verbatim], and "the genes don't matter the metabolism does" [verbatim].
- **Fuel.** Brain insulin resistance means "your neurons are starving for energy" [verbatim]. "Type 3 diabetes" is a real research hypothesis (de la Monte, 2005–2008) [literature, C].
- **Power outage.** Mitochondrial dysfunction comes before amyloid (Swerdlow & Khan's mitochondrial cascade hypothesis, 2004) [literature, C]. Reduced glucose uptake on FDG-PET is real [literature].
- **Fire.** Microglial activation; peripheral CRP and IL-6 predict decline [literature, C].
- **Where he overreaches** [literature]:
 - APOE4 is the strongest common genetic risk factor. Genes *do* matter, and metabolism modifies them.
 - Amyloid-lowering antibodies produced modest but real slowing in early AD, so amyloid is not merely a "tombstone."
 - Critics graded his claims: "Alzheimer's is 100% preventable" and "dementia is reversible" were flagged. Only 16.7% of 48 grouped claims in his Alzheimer's video were fully supported by human evidence, and 0% of the peptide-specific dementia claims (Peptides Weekly Evidence Audit #001).
- **What *is* proven** [literature]: the 2024 Lancet Commission estimates about **45%** of dementia cases are linked to 14 modifiable risk factors:
 - less education, hearing loss, high LDL, depression, traumatic brain injury, physical inactivity, diabetes;
 - smoking, hypertension, obesity, excess alcohol, social isolation, air pollution, untreated vision loss.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Systemic insulin resistance leads to hyperinsulinaemia, then less brain insulin signalling (IRS-1 serine phosphorylation).** Metaphor: the brain's fuel door jams [literature, C].
2. **Lower neuronal glucose use and ATP leads to GSK-3β overactivity and tau hyperphosphorylation.** Metaphor: the scaffolding inside neurons collapses when the power drops [literature, D/C].
3. **Mitochondrial ROS and impaired autophagy mean misfolded Aβ and tau aren't cleared.** Metaphor: the garbage trucks stop running [literature, C/D].
4. **Deep sleep normally expands the interstitial space and CSF flushes metabolites.** He calls it "your brain's pressure washer" [verbatim]. A single night of sleep deprivation measurably raises CSF amyloid in humans [literature, B].
5. **Microglia and complement activation lead to synapse loss.** Metaphor: the immune crew starts dismantling the wiring [literature, C/D].
6. **Vascular damage (hypertension, diabetes, smoking) leads to small-vessel disease and mixed dementia.** Metaphor: the supply roads crumble [literature, A for risk-factor links].

### Tier 0: foundations (this tier carries the evidence)
**Exercise**
- 150–300 min/week moderate aerobic plus resistance training 2–3×/week. Associated with lower risk; small hippocampal-volume gains in RCTs [literature, B].
- He prescribes HIIT 5×/week plus daily strength [his framing]. Scale that to your fitness and your physician's clearance.

**Multidomain programmes**
- The FINGER trial and US POINTER (2025) improved cognition in at-risk older adults with a combined package: exercise, diet, cognitive training and vascular monitoring [literature, B].

**Sleep**
- 7–9 h. **Screen for and treat sleep apnoea.**
- Dark, cool room at about 18–19 °C. He uses 65–67 °F [his framing].
- Fixed schedule.

**Blood pressure**
- Home target under 130/80 if your physician agrees. Intensive BP control lowered MCI in SPRINT MIND [literature, A/B].

**Hearing and vision**
- Get tested. Use hearing aids if needed: in ACHIEVE, they slowed decline in the high-risk subgroup [literature, B].
- Correct vision problems (cataract and similar).

**Diet**
- Mediterranean/MIND-style [literature, C; the MIND RCT was null versus a calorie-matched control].
- He prescribes carnivore [his framing]. That has **no** dementia evidence and is not appropriate in kidney disease.

**Metabolic health**
- Everything in §1. Diabetes roughly doubles risk [literature, C (observational, consistent)].

**Cognitive and social engagement**
- Learn a language or instrument, spatial games, regular social contact [his framing + literature, C].
- He specifically says *not* video games [his framing].

**Avoid**
- Smoking.
- Alcohol above about 14 units/week (none is best).
- Head injury: helmets, fall prevention.
- Chronic anticholinergic burden. **Review with the prescriber; never stop on your own.**

- **HE:** שינה היא לא מנוחה — היא משמרת לילה של צוות תיקונים. כדור שינה מפיל אתכם, אבל הצוות לא מגיע. חלק מתרופות השינה במרשם מבוססות ראיות — לעולם אל תפסיקו אחת מהן על דעת עצמכם.

### Big-Three routing
- **Power outage.** He says "when the power plant is broken the repair window is useless" [verbatim].
- **Cortisol rhythm and inflammation (fire).** In his model, nocturia and early waking come from a cortisol spike at about 2 a.m. and a flat or inverted curve, or from not working hard enough during the day [his framing, paraphrase].
- **Fuel.** High glucose drives night-time urination; insulin resistance and obesity drive sleep apnoea [literature, A].
- **Literature view.**
 - Chronic insomnia is a hyperarousal disorder. **CBT-I is first-line (A).**
 - Nocturia has specific causes:
 - nocturnal polyuria (evening fluids, oedema redistribution, heart failure, diuretics);
 - small bladder capacity (BPH, overactive bladder);
 - **obstructive sleep apnoea** (a natriuretic peptide surge);
 - diabetes.
 - His point that anticholinergic bladder and allergy drugs carry dementia risk is supported by observational data [literature, C].
- **The spiral:** poor sleep raises insulin resistance and appetite, which drives weight gain, which worsens apnoea, which worsens sleep.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Process S (adenosine sleep pressure) and Process C (SCN circadian clock) must line up.** Metaphor: the tank fills during the day and the clock opens the door at night [literature].
2. **Morning light hits melanopsin retinal ganglion cells and anchors the SCN clock (melatonin timing, cortisol awakening response).** Metaphor: setting the master clock every morning. He uses "sunlight… in your eyes" within 15 minutes of waking [his framing] [literature, B].
3. **Hyperarousal (HPA and sympathetic activation, cognitive arousal) delays sleep onset.** Metaphor: the alarm system is armed at bedtime [literature, A for insomnia models].
4. **Deep sleep (slow-wave) drives the GH pulse and glymphatic flow.** Metaphor: the night shift's heavy-lifting hours [literature].
5. **In obstructive apnoea, airway collapse causes negative intrathoracic pressure, which stretches the heart's atria and releases ANP, which makes urine.** Metaphor: the heart thinks there's too much fluid and orders a bathroom run [literature, A].
6. **Evening fluid, alcohol and caffeine all add night-time urine volume** [literature].

**Alcohol**

**Screens**

**Exercise**
- Daily, ideally before evening. He frames poor sleep and nocturia partly as "not working hard enough during the day" [his framing, paraphrase].

**Dinner**
- Finish 2–3 h before bed.

# 9. Cortisol / chronic stress / circadian disruption

### Hook
- **EN:** Cortisol is "the emergency brake hormone." [his framing, verbatim] Pull it all day and you go nowhere.
- **HE:** קורטיזול הוא בלם החירום. מי שמושך בו כל היום — לא נוסע לשום מקום.

### Big-Three routing
- **Fire.** In his framing, chronic cortisol "suppresses immune tolerance" and causes systemic inflammation [paraphrase]. Literature: chronic stress leads to glucocorticoid *resistance*, which allows inflammation [literature, B].
- **Fuel.** Cortisol raises glucose production and insulin resistance; visceral fat follows [literature, A].
- **Power outage.** Cortisol blunts the GH pulse via somatostatin, wrecks deep sleep and drives muscle breakdown [his framing + literature, B].
- **The gut link** [his framing]: "a weird microbiome keeps your cortisol high" [verbatim]. The literature shows bidirectional HPA-microbiome signalling in animal and early human work [literature, C/D].
- **Literature caveat.**
 - "Adrenal fatigue" is not a recognised diagnosis (Endocrine Society).
 - True cortisol *disease* (Cushing's, Addison's) is rare, serious and diagnosed with specific tests.
 - Most "high cortisol" is behavioural and circadian, so it's fixable.
- **The spiral:** stress → poor sleep → higher evening cortisol → insulin resistance and cravings → weight gain and fatigue → more stress.

### Mechanism chain
1. **Perceived threat → hypothalamic CRH → pituitary ACTH → adrenal cortisol.** Metaphor: dispatch calls the fire department [literature].
2. **Normal rhythm:** a cortisol awakening response 30–45 min after waking, then a fall to a low point around midnight. Metaphor: a ramp up in the morning, a glide down at night. He describes a peak at about 8 a.m. and a near-flat line at midnight [his framing] [literature].
3. **Morning light and activity sharpen the peak; evening light, caffeine and inbox work push the curve flat and late.** Metaphor: you keep dispatching trucks after the fire is out [literature, B].
4. **High evening cortisol and sympathetic tone suppress slow-wave sleep and GH.** Metaphor: the night shift never starts [literature, B].
5. **Chronic glucocorticoid exposure → glucose production, visceral fat, muscle protein breakdown, hippocampal stress.** Metaphor: the body rationing for a siege that never ends [literature, A at pathological levels; B for chronic stress].
6. **Glucocorticoid receptor resistance in immune cells → inflammation escapes control.** Metaphor: the brake cable stretches until the brake stops working [literature, B].

**Wake time and light**

- Keep at least 1–2 easy days.

**Downshift practice**
- Slow breathing (about 6 breaths/min) or box breathing, 5 min twice daily [literature, B].
- 10 min of daily walking outdoors without a phone.

**Sleep**
- 7–9 h. **Same schedule on weekends** to avoid social jet lag.

**Meals**
- Regular, protein-anchored. Avoid very-low-calorie dieting during high-stress periods.
- He uses a 2-week carnivore "reset" [his framing]. No evidence beyond **D/C**, and not appropriate for kidney disease.

**Alcohol**
- None or minimal. It raises night cortisol and fragments sleep.

**Environment audit (his "lifestyle stress audit") [his framing]**

- Caffeine over 400–600 mg/day?
- Overtraining?
- "White-knuckling" work stress?
- His instruction is to fix the baseline first. That matches literature priorities.

# 10. Migraines

### Hook
- **EN:** "The trigger changes… the kindling is always the same." [his framing, verbatim]
- **HE:** הטריגר משתנה כל פעם. חומר הבעירה — תמיד אותו דבר.

### Big-Three routing
- **What the corpus has.** Only his reframe; the "solution" is withheld behind a comment-for-DM call to action.
 - "It's an electrical storm in the cortex… but the lightning strike doesn't start in the head… it begins in the gut" [his framing, verbatim].
 - The kindling is "mitochondrial failure, neuronal excitability, neuroinflammation" [his framing, verbatim].
 - The protocol below is therefore **[inferred]** from his framework plus the literature.
- **Power outage** [literature, B/C]. Migraine brains show reduced mitochondrial energy reserve on MR spectroscopy. That is exactly why **riboflavin** and **CoQ10** became preventives.
- **Fire** [literature, A]. Neurogenic inflammation: the trigeminal nerve releases **CGRP**, causing vasodilation and sensitisation. Blocking CGRP is the most important migraine advance in decades.
- **Fuel** [literature, B]. Fasting and skipped meals are well-documented triggers. Insulin resistance and obesity are associated with progression to chronic migraine.
- **Gut** [his framing]. Literature: migraine is associated with IBS, coeliac disease and *H. pylori* [literature, C]. Gut-to-brain causation is unproven.
- **The spiral:**
 1. Low energy reserve makes the cortex excitable.
 2. Excitability allows cortical spreading depolarisation (the aura).
 3. That activates the trigeminal system and CGRP inflammation.
 4. Inflammation causes sensitisation, which lowers the threshold for the next attack (chronification).

### Mechanism chain
1. **Neuronal energy reserve is low (lower phosphocreatine/ATP on MR spectroscopy).** Metaphor: the grid runs with no spare capacity [literature, B].
2. **Cortical hyperexcitability (glutamate up, ion-channel variants in familial hemiplegic migraine).** Metaphor: dry kindling, where any spark catches [literature].
3. **Cortical spreading depolarisation, a slow wave of depolarisation that produces the aura.** Metaphor: the "electrical storm in the cortex" (his image, accurate here) [literature].
4. **The trigeminovascular system fires and releases CGRP and PACAP.** Metaphor: the alarm chemical floods the blood vessels around the brain [literature, A].
5. **Peripheral and then central sensitisation (allodynia).** Metaphor: the volume knob on pain is stuck high, which is why early treatment works better [literature, A].
6. **Frequent painkiller use → medication-overuse headache.** Metaphor: the fire extinguisher becomes fuel [literature, A].

### Tier 0: foundations (regularity is the core)
**Sleep**
- Same sleep and wake time 7/7. Both short sleep and weekend lie-ins trigger attacks [literature, B].

**Meals**
- **Regular meals every 4–5 h while awake. No skipped meals and no long fasting windows.**
- This deliberately diverges from his 18:6 framing used elsewhere: fasting is a classic migraine trigger.

**Hydration**
- About 30–35 mL/kg/day.
- **Exception: heart or kidney fluid restriction, or low sodium, where the physician sets the volume.**

**Exercise**
- **Aerobic exercise 40 min, 3×/week.** One RCT found it about as effective as topiramate for prevention [literature, B].
- Build up gradually: sudden intense exertion can trigger attacks.

**Caffeine**
- Consistent and modest: ≤200 mg/day, same time daily.
- Avoid daily swings, which cause withdrawal headaches.

**Alcohol**
- Minimal, especially red wine if it's a personal trigger.

**Headache diary**
- Days, severity, acute medications used, sleep, meals, menstrual cycle, triggers.
- **Count the days you take acute painkillers.**

**Weight**
- If BMI is high, losing weight lowers attack frequency [literature, B].

**Screens and light**
- Breaks every 45–60 min; manage glare.

**Recheck cadence.** Diary continuously, reviewed monthly. Any preventive (supplement or drug) is judged after **8–12 weeks** at a stable dose.

**If/then rules**
- **Acute painkillers on ≥10 days/month (triptans/combinations) or ≥15 days/month (simple analgesics)** → physician (medication-overuse management). Don't white-knuckle a withdrawal alone.
- **≥4 migraine days/month, or attacks that are disabling** → physician to discuss prevention.
- **Aura lasting more than 60 min, motor weakness during the aura, or first-ever aura after 40** → physician promptly (urgently if ongoing).
- **Starting magnesium above 350 mg, feverfew, or any supplement while pregnant or planning pregnancy** → physician first.
- **Migraine with aura on estrogen-containing contraception** → physician review.

### Checkpoints
Judge preventives at 8–12 weeks, not before.
- **Week 2:** diary running; sleep and meal regularity locked; caffeine stable.
- **Week 4:** first monthly count of migraine days and acute-medication days (baseline vs now); exercise 3×/week started.
- **Week 8:** riboflavin/magnesium/CoQ10 at about two-thirds of the evaluation window; monthly migraine days trending down? Acute-medication days under the overuse limit?
- **Week 12:** compare monthly migraine days with baseline. Below 50% reduction → physician review of Tier 2 prevention.

### Red flags: stop and see a doctor
**Emergency for any of the following ("SNOOP4")**
- **Thunderclap headache** (peak within 1 min).
- First or worst headache of your life.
- Headache with fever, stiff neck or rash.
- New neurological deficit: weakness, speech loss, vision loss, confusion.
- Headache after head trauma.
- New headache after age 50.
- Headache that wakes you or worsens lying down or when straining.
- Papilloedema or pulsatile tinnitus (raised intracranial pressure).
- New headache in pregnancy or postpartum.
- Headache in someone with cancer or immunosuppression.
- A clear change in a long-standing headache pattern.
