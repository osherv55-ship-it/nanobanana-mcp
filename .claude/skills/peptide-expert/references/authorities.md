# Expert lenses: independent authorities (rank by conflict-of-interest)

Use these as the **credibility counterweight to vendor/influencer voices** (Dean Henry et al.).
Core rule the skill enforces: **the more someone sells peptides, the more their enthusiasm is
discounted.** When a vendor and an independent scientist disagree, weight the scientist. Present
BOTH voices to Osher when relevant — "the seller says X, the independent scientist/skeptic says Y."

Independence ladder (most → least independent within peptides):
**academic researchers** (no consumer product) → **independent physicians/communicators** (sell
media/practice, NOT peptides) → **educators who train/sell** (Seeds) → **vendors/clinics** (Dean
Henry, Koniver, Campbell — revenue tied directly to peptide uptake).

---

## Tier 1 — Academic / clinical researchers (the real source; go here for MECHANISM)
These are the scientists behind the peptides everyone else talks about. Conflicts, where they
exist, are patents/pharma grants — **not consumer sales**. For scientific truth, cite THEIR
peer-reviewed work over any podcast.

- **Predrag Šikirić** (Univ. of Zagreb) — THE **BPC-157** researcher; dozens of preclinical
  studies (GI healing, tendon/ligament, neuroprotection, organ protection). Academic, no retail
  brand. Caveat: almost all BPC-157 evidence is HIS rodent work — great depth, but preclinical and
  single-group-heavy (note when citing).
- **Daniel Drucker** (Univ. of Toronto) — foundational **GLP-1 / incretin** biology (the science
  under Ozempic/semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide). Pharma ties (disclosed), no consumer sales.
- **Ania Jastreboff** (Yale), **Filip Knop** (Copenhagen) — clinical GLP-1/incretin trialists.
- **Loren Pickart** (UCSF) — discoverer of **GHK-Cu** (copper peptide, skin/wound). IP/royalty
  interest, not an influencer.
- **Allan Goldstein** (George Washington U.) — **thymosin** peptides (thymosin α1, **thymosin β4 /
  TB-500**), immune modulation. Co-inventor/pharma, no biohacker brand.
- **Sami Said** — co-discoverer of **VIP**.
- **Kun Cheng** (UMKC) + authors of major peer-reviewed therapeutic-peptide reviews — peptide drug
  design/delivery, no retail presence. Good neutral overviews.
- ⚠️ **Vladimir Khavinson** (Russia) — **bioregulators / Epitalon** lineage. Scientifically central
  BUT **significant IP/commercial association** — products are marketed as "Khavinson peptides."
  Treat his bioregulator/anti-aging/organ-specific claims as **tier C/D with a conflict flag**, not
  established fact. (This is exactly what Dean Henry's thyroid/Alzheimer bioregulator claims lean on.)
- Semax/Cortexin: evidence is concentrated in **Russian academic groups** (stroke trials) — real
  but geographically concentrated, thin Western replication.

## Tier 2 — Independent physicians / communicators (do NOT sell peptides)
The credible "second opinion." They monetize media/practice, **not peptide vials** — so when they
say "evidence is thin, be careful," it's trustworthy (no upside for them in you using peptides).

- **Peter Attia, MD** — longevity physician (*Outlive*). Rigorous, trial-first, risk–benefit
  framing; explicitly cautious about unregulated "research peptides." Best lens for **should I even
  do this / how risky**. Conflict: practice, book, paid media — not peptides.
- **Andrew Huberman, PhD** (Stanford neuroscience) — Huberman Lab. Explains **BPC-157, GH
  secretagogues (ipamorelin/sermorelin), GLP-1, MOTS-c** with references; flags regulatory
  uncertainty and missing human data. Conflict: podcast sponsors/general supplements — no peptide
  brand. Best lens for **mechanism-made-accessible + honest evidence caveats**.
- **Rhonda Patrick, PhD** — evidence-heavy, mostly on **approved** peptide drugs (GLP-1). Cautious.

## Tier 3 — Educators who train/sell (moderate–high conflict)
- **William Seeds, MD** — wrote the clinician "peptide protocols" playbook; runs peptide-therapy
  **certification/courses**. Influential and clinically detailed, but his brand runs on peptide
  enthusiasm — weight accordingly.

## Tier 4 — Vendors / clinics / influencers (max conflict) — see dean-henry.md
Dean Henry, Craig Koniver, Jay Campbell, compounding clinics, research-peptide sites. Revenue
depends on you buying/using. Useful for practical scaffolding; treat claims as marketing until
cross-checked against Tier 1–2.

## How the agent uses this
- **Mechanism / "is it real"** → lead with Tier 1 (the actual researchers) + neutral reviews.
- **"Should I, and how risky"** → Tier 2 (Attia/Huberman) — the skeptic voice.
- **"What do practitioners do"** → Tier 3–4, always with the conflict flag.
- When Osher cites a vendor claim, **name the independent counter-voice**: e.g. Dean Henry's
  bioregulator-for-thyroid claim ↔ Khavinson's own IP conflict + absence of Tier-2 endorsement.
- Never present influencer enthusiasm as consensus. The skill's job is to show Osher the gap
  between "seller says" and "independent science says," and let the evidence tier decide.
